**CT CHEST WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 68-year-old female, immunocompromised, presenting with haemoptysis. Evaluate for pulmonary embolism versus infection.

**Technique:** Helical MDCT acquisition was performed from the thoracic inlet through the adrenal glands following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The lungs demonstrate several focal areas of consolidation within the right upper lobe and left lower lobe. Specifically, there is a nodular area of consolidation in the right upper lobe surrounded by a characteristic "halo sign," consisting of a rim of ground-glass opacity. Within this consolidated region, there is an eccentric, contrast-enhancing focal outpouching arising from a segmental branch of the pulmonary artery, measuring approximately 4 mm, consistent with a pseudoaneurysm. There is no evidence of large central pulmonary embolism; the main, lobar, and segmental pulmonary arteries remain patent throughout. No pleural effusions or pneumothorax are identified.

The mediastinum is unremarkable. The heart size is within normal limits and there is no pericardial effusion. The trachea and mainstem bronchi are patent. No significant mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy is noted.

Regarding incidental findings, there are small, stable calcified granulomas in the bilateral lung bases, likely sequelae of prior healed infections. Mild degenerative changes are noted in the visualized thoracic spine with marginal osteophyte formation. The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and spleen, appear unremarkable for the patient's age.

**Conclusion:**
Findings are highly suggestive of pulmonary angioinvasive aspergillosis, complicated by a pulmonary artery pseudoaneurysm. No evidence of pulmonary embolism.
