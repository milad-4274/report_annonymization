**INDICATION:** 57-year-old male involved in a high-speed motor vehicle accident (driver's seat). Presents with acute chest pain. Evaluate for pneumothorax, rib fractures, and aortic dissection.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multidetector CT of the chest was performed with intravenous contrast administration. Axial images were obtained with multiplanar reconstructions.

**FINDINGS:**
The lungs are clear without evidence of focal consolidation or suspicious pulmonary nodules. There is no evidence of pneumothorax or pleural effusion bilaterally. The tracheobronchial tree is patent and unremarkable.

Regarding the cardiovascular structures, the thoracic aorta maintains a normal caliber throughout its course; there is no evidence of intimal flap, dissection, or traumatic aneurysm. However, there is a focal, contrast-filled outpouching arising from the wall of the main pulmonary artery, measuring approximately 1.2 cm in maximum dimension, consistent with a traumatic pseudoaneurysm. The right and left pulmonary arteries are otherwise patent. The heart size is within normal limits, and the pericardium is thin without effusion.

The mediastinum is midline. There is no significant lymphadenopathy identified in the hilar or mediastinal stations. The esophagus is unremarkable.

Evaluation of the thoracic cage reveals multiple acute, minimally displaced fractures of the left lateral 4th through 7th ribs. The sternum remains intact. The visualized portions of the shoulder girdles are unremarkable.

Incidental findings include mild degenerative changes of the thoracic spine with small marginal osteophytes. There is also a small, stable 4mm calcified granuloma in the right lower lobe, which is non-specific.

**CONCLUSION:**
Traumatic pseudoaneurysm of the main pulmonary artery. No associated hemothorax.
