**INDICATION:** 72-year-old female presenting with epigastric pain for three days. Laboratory results show elevated lipase and CRP. Evaluate for pancreatitis and potential complications.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the abdomen and pelvis was performed following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous attenuation; no focal masses or intrahepatic biliary dilatation is seen. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The common bile duct is within normal limits for age. The spleen is normal in size and attenuation.

Regarding the pancreas, there is diffuse enlargement of the pancreatic parenchyma with significant peripancreatic stranding and fluid collections extending into the anterior pararenal space. There is heterogeneous enhancement of the gland, though no discrete areas of liquefactive necrosis are identified at this time. Within the course of the splenic artery, just proximal to the splenic hilum, there is a focal, contrast-enhancing saccular dilation measuring approximately 8 mm, consistent with a pseudoaneurysm.

The adrenal glands are unremarkable. The kidneys show symmetric excretion of contrast without hydronephrosis or nephrolithiasis. A small amount of simple cortical cysts are noted in the left kidney, which are benign in appearance.

The gastrointestinal tract is non-dilated. There is no evidence of free intraperitoneal air or high-grade bowel obstruction. The urinary bladder is partially filled and appears smooth-walled. Small, stable atherosclerotic calcifications are noted along the abdominal aorta. There is mild degenerative change involving the lumbar spine with marginal osteophytes.

No significant lymphadenopathy is identified in the retroperitoneum or pelvic regions. No pleural effusions are seen in the visualized lung bases.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are consistent with acute pancreatitis. There is an associated splenic artery pseudoaneurysm. No evidence of pancreatic necrosis or abscess formation.
