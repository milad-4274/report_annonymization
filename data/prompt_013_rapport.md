**INDICATION:** 44-year-old male presenting with acute abdominal pain and anemia following a recent percutaneous liver biopsy. Evaluate for hematoma or active hemorrhage.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multidetector CT of the abdomen and pelvis was performed. Images were acquired in the portal venous phase following the administration of intravenous contrast. Coronal and sagittal reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour. There is a focal, well-circumscribed contrast-enhancing sac measuring approximately 8 mm in diameter arising from a distal branch of the right hepatic artery, consistent with a pseudoaneurysm at the site of the previous biopsy tract. There is an associated 4 cm peripherally enhancing subcapsular hematoma in the right lobe of the liver. No evidence of active extravasation of contrast material into the peritoneum or the hepatic parenchyma is seen. The gallbladder is unremarkable without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The portal vein and main hepatic veins remain patent.

The spleen, pancreas, and adrenal glands are normal in appearance. Both kidneys demonstrate symmetric enhancement and excretion without hydronephrosis or nephrolithiasis. A small, 5 mm simple cyst is noted in the lower pole of the left kidney, which is an incidental finding.

The gastrointestinal tract is non-dilated. There is no free intraperitoneal air. A small amount of simple free fluid is noted in the paracolic gutters, likely reactive. The urinary bladder is moderately distended and unremarkable.

The visualized portions of the lower lungs are clear. No significant lymphadenopathy is identified in the retroperitoneum or pelvic regions. The visualized osseous structures show no acute fractures; there is mild degenerative change noted at the L4-L5 facet joints.

**CONCLUSION:**
Right hepatic artery pseudoaneurysm with an associated right hepatic subcapsular hematoma. No evidence of active bleeding.
