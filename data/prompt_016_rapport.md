**INDICATION:** 22-year-old female involved in an electric bike crash. Positive FAST exam for free fluid and initial hemodynamic instability. Evaluate for traumatic lesions.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multidetector CT of the abdomen and pelvis was performed with intravenous contrast administration. Coronal and sagittal reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and attenuation without evidence of laceration, contour irregularity, or intrahepatic hematoma. The gallbladder is unremarkable and no biliary ductal dilatation is noted.

There is a high-grade injury involving the spleen. There is a deep parenchymal laceration extending through more than 25% of the splenic parenchyma, with associated segmental devascularization of the superior pole. A large perisplenic hematoma is present, measuring approximately 6 cm in maximum dimension, with active extravasation of contrast material noted into the splenic parenchyma. These findings are consistent with an AAST Grade 4 splenic injury.

The pancreas is normal in morphology and enhancement; the pancreatic duct is not dilated. Both adrenal glands appear symmetric and unremarkable.

Both kidneys demonstrate normal cortical thickness and symmetric excretion of contrast. No perinephric hematomas or urinary extravasation identified.

The gastrointestinal tract is unremarkable. There is no evidence of bowel wall thickening or pneumoperitoneum. The appendix is visualized and appears normal.

A moderate amount of free fluid is noted within the peritoneal cavity, primarily localized to the left upper quadrant and the paracolic gutters, consistent with hemoperitoneum.

The aorta and its major branches are patent and maintain normal caliber. There is a small, benign-appearing simple cyst in the left kidney, measuring 8 mm, which is an incidental finding. Additionally, there is a mild prominence of the pelvic venous plexus, likely physiological given the patient's age and sex.

The visualized portions of the lower thoracic cage show no acute fractures. The pelvic ring remains intact.

**CONCLUSION:**
AAST grade 4 splenic injury with associated hemoperitoneum and evidence of active contrast extravasation.
