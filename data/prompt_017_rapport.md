**EXAMINATION:** CT Angiography (CTA) Abdomen and Pelvis

**INDICATION:** 36-year-old female with persistent hypotension following cesarean section. Evaluate for hematoma or active hemorrhage.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multidetector CT imaging was performed through the abdomen and pelvis. Intravenous contrast was administered via a power injector with arterial phase timing optimized for vascular assessment. Coronal and sagittal reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and attenuation without focal lesions or contour abnormality. The gallbladder is unremarkable without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen, pancreas, and adrenal glands are normal in appearance. There is no evidence of free intraperitoneal air or high-attenuation fluid within the peritoneal cavity.

The kidneys show symmetric enhancement and excretion. No hydronephrosis or renal calculi are identified. The aorta and its major branches are patent throughout their course.

Within the pelvis, the uterus is enlarged, consistent with recent pregnancy. There is an extensive amount of heterogeneous material within the uterine cavity and surrounding the myometrium, consistent with a large intrauterine and perimetrial hematoma. During the arterial phase, there is a distinct jet of contrast extravasation originating from a branch of the right uterine artery, indicating active intra-uterine bleeding. The left uterine artery appears patent without evidence of extravasation.

The urinary bladder is partially filled and unremarkable. Small, benign-appearing nabothian cysts are noted within the cervix. There is a small amount of physiologic free fluid in the pouch of Douglas. The visualized bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall enhancement. No lymphadenopathy is seen in the pelvic or retroperitoneal chains. The visualized osseous structures are intact.

**CONCLUSION:**
Active extravasation of contrast from the right uterine artery into a large intrauterine and perimetrial hematoma.
