**INDICATION:** 89-year-old female presenting with non-traumatic right flank pain. Evaluate for pyelonephritis versus hematoma.

**TECHNIQUE:** Computed tomography of the abdomen and pelvis was performed with intravenous contrast administered. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour without focal mass lesions; however, there is mild hepatic steatosis noted. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The biliary tree is not dilated. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance.

Regarding the kidneys, both show normal enhancement and symmetric size. There is no evidence of hydronephrosis, nephrolithiasis, or perinephric stranding to suggest acute pyelonephritis. The adrenal glands are unremarkable.

Within the retroperitoneum, there is a large, hyperdense collection involving the right psoas muscle, extending from the lumbar spine down toward the inguinal region. This collection is consistent with a psoas hematoma. Following administration of IV contrast, there is an area of focal, high-attenuation contrast extravasation within the center of the hematoma, indicating active bleeding. The left psoas muscle is normal in caliber and attenuation.

The gastrointestinal tract is non-dilated. There are several small, benign-appearing diverticula noted throughout the descending and sigmoid colon. The urinary bladder is partially filled and unremarkable.

The visualized pelvic organs are stable for age. There is mild degenerative change noted in the lumbar spine with marginal osteophytes and mild disc space narrowing. No free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites is identified.

**CONCLUSION:**
Large right psoas muscle hematoma with evidence of active contrast extravasation. No signs of pyelonephritis.
