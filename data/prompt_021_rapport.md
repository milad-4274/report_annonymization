**INDICATION:** 32-year-old male presenting with chest pain. Evaluate for pulmonary embolism.

**TECHIQUE:** Computed tomography of the chest was performed with intravenous contrast administered via a power injector. Angiographic phases were obtained to evaluate the pulmonary arteries. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The thoracic aorta and its primary branches are normal in caliber without evidence of aneurysm or dissection. The heart size is within normal limits. There is no pericardial effusion.

Regarding the pulmonary vasculature, there is no evidence of filling defects within the main, lobar, segmental, or subsegmental pulmonary arteries to suggest pulmonary embolism. The pulmonary trunk is normal in diameter.

Within the lung parenchyma, there is a well-circumscribed, rounded nodule measuring approximately 1.8 cm located in the right upper lobe. This lesion is situated immediately adjacent to a segmental branch of the pulmonary artery. The nodule demonstrates characteristic internal fat attenuation and small, popcorn-like calcifications, which is highly suggestive of a pulmonary hamartoma. The surrounding lung parenchyma is clear, with no consolidation, pleural effusions, or pneumothorax.

The mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes are not enlarged by size criteria. The esophagus is unremarkable. There is a small, benign-appearing pericardial fat pad noted, which is a common normal variant. Additionally, there is mild thickening of the ligamentum arteriosum, appearing as a thin soft tissue band, which is an incidental finding. The visualized portions of the upper abdomen, including the liver and adrenal glands, are unremarkable. The bony structures of the thoracic cage show no acute fractures.

**CONCLUSION:**
There is a pulmonary hamartoma in the right upper lobe adjacent to a pulmonary artery; there are no associated complications such as obstructive pneumonia or hemorrhage.
