**INDICATION:** 23-year-old female presenting with chest pain. Evaluate for pulmonary embolism.

**TECHIQUE:** Computed tomography of the chest was performed with intravenous contrast administered via a power injector. Bolus tracking was utilized to optimize opacification of the pulmonary arteries. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The lungs are clear without evidence of focal consolidation, pleural effusion, or pneumothorax. There is no evidence of pulmonary embolism; the main, lobar, segmental, and subsegmental pulmonary arteries are patent throughout. The tracheobronchial tree is patent and normal in caliber.

Regarding the pulmonary vasculature, there is an anomalous drainage pattern noted. Specifically, the right upper lobe pulmonary veins do not drain into the left atrium but instead course anteriorly and inferiorly to drain into the superior vena cava. The remaining pulmonary veins from the left lung and the right lower lobe demonstrate normal anatomy, draining appropriately into the left atrium. This is consistent with a partial anomalous pulmonary venous return.

The heart size is within normal limits. There is no pericardial effusion. The ascending aorta and descending thoracic aorta are normal in diameter without dissection or aneurysm. The mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes are not enlarged.

Incidental findings include a small, benign-appearing pericardial fat pad. Additionally, there is a prominent azygos vein, which is a common normal variant. The visualized portions of the upper abdomen, including the liver and adrenal glands, are unremarkable. The bony structures of the thoracic cage are intact without acute fracture.

**CONCLUSION:**
Partial anomalous pulmonary venous return involving the right upper lobe pulmonary veins. No associated signs of right heart strain or volume overload.
