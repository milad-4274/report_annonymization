**INDICATION:** 55-year-old male presenting with chronic cough and weight loss. Evaluate for tuberculosis versus lung malignancy.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT images were acquired through the chest without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The lungs are clear of any acute airspace consolidation or suspicious pulmonary nodules. There is no evidence of an active mass or pleural effusion. In the right upper lobe, there are stable fibrotic bands and linear scarring extending toward the pleura, consistent with sequelae of prior primary tuberculosis. There is mild associated volume loss in the right apex, but no evidence of cavitation, bronchial dilation, or current inflammatory activity. The left lung is clear without focal lesions.

The mediastinum is unremarkable. The trachea and mainstem bronchi are patent. There is no significant mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy. The heart size is within normal limits and the pericardium is thin. The thoracic aorta shows mild atherosclerotic calcification at the arch.

The visualized upper abdominal organs are unremarkable. There is a small, simple hepatic cyst measuring approximately 1 cm in the right lobe of the liver, which is an incidental finding. The pleural spaces are clear. The bony structures of the thoracic cage show mild degenerative changes of the thoracic spine.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are consistent with post-primary lung tuberculosis. There are no associated complications.
