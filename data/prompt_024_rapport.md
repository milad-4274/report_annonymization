**CT CHEST WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 68-year-old female, immunocompromised, presenting with haemoptysis. Evaluate for pulmonary embolism versus infection.

**Technique:** Helical CT acquisition was performed from the thoracic inlet through the adrenal glands following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are visualized. There is a focal area of consolidation in the right upper lobe measuring approximately 2.4 cm in maximum dimension. This lesion is surrounded by a peripheral zone of ground-glass opacity, creating a classic "halo sign." No evidence of cavitation, air-fluid levels, or associated pleural effusion is seen in this region. The remainder of the lung parenchyma is clear, without evidence of diffuse interstitial disease or other nodules.

The pulmonary arteries are patent throughout. There is no evidence of filling defects within the main, lobar, or segmental pulmonary arteries to suggest pulmonary embolism.

The heart size is within normal limits. The pericardium is thin and unremarkable. The thoracic aorta and great vessels demonstrate normal caliber and course.

The mediastinal and hilar contours are stable. There is no significant lymphadenopathy identified. The esophagus is midline and normal in caliber.

Regarding the surrounding structures, there is mild degenerative change noted in the thoracic spine with small marginal osteophytes. A few small, benign-appearing calcified granulomas are noted in the left lower lobe, likely sequelae of prior healed infection. The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and spleen, are unremarkable for a study of this technique.

**Conclusion:**
Findings are consistent with pulmonary angioinvasive aspergillosis. There are no associated complications such as cavitation or hemorrhage.
