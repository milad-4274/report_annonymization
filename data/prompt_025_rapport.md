**CT CHEST WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 78-year-old male with suspicion of superior vena cava syndrome. Evaluate for SVC thrombosis or extrinsic compression.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the thoracic inlet through the adrenal glands following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were performed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are clear without evidence of focal consolidation, suspicious pulmonary nodules, or pleural effusions. There is no pneumothorax. Mild atherosclerotic calcifications are noted within the aortic arch and the descending thoracic aorta. Small, stable calcified granulomas are seen in the right lower lobe, likely representing old healed inflammatory disease.

There is a very large, lobulated soft tissue mass centered within the anterior and middle mediastinum. This mass measures approximately 12 x 8 x 15 cm and extends from the thoracic inlet down to the level of the carina. The mass completely encases the great vessels; however, there is maintained patency of the superior vena cava. While the SVC is significantly narrowed due to external compression by the lymphomatous mass, there is no evidence of intraluminal thrombus, and contrast opacification persists throughout the vessel. The ascending aorta and pulmonary arteries are displaced but remain patent.

The heart size is within normal limits for the patient's age. The pericardium is thin without effusion. The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and spleen, are unremarkable. Bony structures show mild degenerative changes of the thoracic spine and some osteophytic spurring at the T4-T6 levels.

**Conclusion:**
Large mediastinal lymphoma causing significant extrinsic compression of the superior vena cava, without evidence of SVC thrombosis.
