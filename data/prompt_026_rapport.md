**CT CHEST WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 69-year-old female with suspicion of superior vena cava syndrome. Evaluate for SVC thrombosis or compression.

**Technique:** Helical CT acquisition was performed from the thoracic inlet through the adrenal glands following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are clear without evidence of consolidation, pleural effusion, or pneumothorax. There is a large, heterogeneously enhancing soft tissue mass located in the right paramediastinal region. This tumor measures approximately 6.2 x 4.5 cm and demonstrates irregular borders, abutting the mediastinal pleura. Despite its size and proximity to the great vessels, there is no significant narrowing or extrinsic compression of the superior vena cava. The vessel remains patent with normal opacification throughout its course; no endovascular thrombus is identified. The azygos vein and brachiocephalic veins are similarly patent.

The trachea and mainstem bronchi remain midline and patent. The esophagus shows a normal caliber without wall thickening. The heart size is within normal limits, and there is no pericardial effusion. The visualized portions of the upper abdominal organs, including the liver and adrenal glands, are unremarkable.

Incidental findings include mild atherosclerotic calcifications within the aortic arch. There are small, stable calcified granulomas in the left lower lobe, likely sequelae of prior healed infection. Mild degenerative changes are noted in the thoracic spine.

**Conclusion:**
Large right paramediastinal lung tumor without evidence of superior vena cava compression or thrombosis.
