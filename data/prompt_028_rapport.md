**INDICATION:** 24-year-old female presenting after suicide attempt via fall from height. Evaluate for traumatic lesions.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multidetector CT of the chest was performed without intravenous contrast. Axial images were acquired from the lung apices through the upper abdomen. Multiplanar reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The lungs are clear. There is no evidence of pneumothorax or pleural effusion. The pulmonary parenchyma shows no focal consolidation, suspicious nodules, or ground-glass opacities. The tracheobronchial tree is patent and midline.

Regarding the thoracic cage, there are acute, non-displaced fractures involving the right 4th, 5th, and 6th ribs along the mid-axillary line. These appear as serial fractures. There is no associated flail segment. No subcutaneous emphysema is noted in the chest wall. The left ribs and clavicles remain intact. The sternum is unremarkable.

The mediastinum is within normal limits for age. The heart size is normal and there is no pericardial effusion. The aorta and great vessels demonstrate normal caliber and course. The esophagus is unremarkable.

Incidental findings include a small, benign-appearing pericardial fat pad. Additionally, there is a tiny, well-circumscribed pulmonary nodule measuring 2mm in the right lower lobe, likely an intrapulmonary lymph node. The visualized portions of the upper abdomen, including the liver and spleen, are unremarkable.

**CONCLUSION:**
Serial fractures of the right 4th, 5th, and 6th ribs without associated pneumothorax, hemothorax, or pulmonary contusion.
