**INDICATION:** 69-year-old male presenting with epigastric pain for three days. Laboratory results show elevated lipase and CRP. Evaluate for pancreatitis and potential complications.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the abdomen and pelvis was performed following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous attenuation; no focal masses or intrahepatic biliary dilatation is seen. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid, and no radiopaque gallstones are identified. The common bile duct is within normal limits for the patient's age.

The pancreas is diffusely enlarged and edematous, showing mild heterogeneous enhancement. There is moderate stranding of the peripancreatic fat, particularly around the head and body of the gland. However, there are no organized fluid collections, no evidence of walled-off necrosis, and no vascular complications such as pseudoaneurysms or venous thrombosis noted. The pancreatic duct is not dilated.

The spleen and adrenal glands are unremarkable in appearance. The kidneys show symmetric enhancement and excretion without hydronephrosis or suspicious masses. A small, simple cortical cyst is noted in the lower pole of the left kidney, which is an incidental finding.

The gastrointestinal tract is non-dilated. There is no free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites. The aorta is mildly ectatic and tortuous, consistent with the patient's age, but no aneurysm is present. Small, scattered atherosclerotic calcifications are noted along the iliac arteries. The urinary bladder is partially filled and appears smooth-walled. Visualized lung bases are clear.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are consistent with acute pancreatitis. There are no associated complications, such as necrosis or fluid collections.
