**INDICATION:** 81-year-old male presenting with acute onset of sharp abdominal pain. Rule out ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm or mesenteric ischemia.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the abdomen and pelvis was performed with intravenous contrast administration. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The thoracic aorta is normal in caliber without evidence of dissection or aneurysm. Within the abdomen, the abdominal aorta is mildly ectatic but maintains a normal diameter throughout its course; there is no evidence of aneurysmal dilation, rupture, or intramural hematoma. The celiac axis, superior mesenteric artery, and inferior mesenteric artery are patent with an appropriate contrast opacification, showing no signs of high-grade stenosis or occlusion to suggest mesenteric ischemia.

Regarding the gastrointestinal tract, there is a focal defect noted in the anterior wall of the first portion of the duodenum. This is associated with a small amount of extraluminal gas and localized inflammatory stranding in the adjacent retroperitoneum, consistent with a perforated duodenal ulcer. There is a moderate volume of free intraperitoneal air present, most prominent in the anterior peritoneal space and under the diaphragm. A small amount of free fluid is noted in the paracolic gutters. The remainder of the stomach, small bowel, and colon demonstrate normal wall thickness and enhancement patterns. The appendix is visualized and appears unremarkable.

The liver is normal in size and contour with homogeneous parenchymal attenuation. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. The adrenal glands are bilateral and symmetric. Both kidneys show symmetric excretion of contrast without hydronephrosis or nephrolithiasis.

There is mild degenerative change noted in the lumbar spine with marginal osteophytes and mild narrowing of the intervertebral disc spaces, which is expected for the patient's age. Small, benign-appearing hepatic cysts are noted in the right lobe of the liver. No lymphadenopathy is identified in the retroperitoneum or pelvic regions. The urinary bladder is partially filled and unremarkable. No suspicious osseous lesions are seen.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are diagnostic of a perforated duodenal ulcer with associated pneumoperitoneum. No evidence of ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm or mesenteric ischemia.
