**INDICATION:** 47-year-old male presenting after a paragliding crash. Patient reports pelvic and lower back pain. Currently hemodynamically stable. Evaluate for traumatic lesions.

**TECHNIQUE:** Computed tomography of the pelvis was performed. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The visualized portions of the lower thoracic and lumbar spine demonstrate maintained vertebral body heights and alignment. There is no evidence of acute fracture or subluxation in the lumbar region. The spinal canal is patent.

Regarding the pelvic ring, there are multiple fractures identified. Specifically, there are non-displaced fractures involving both superior and inferior pubic rami bilaterally. There is also a minimally displaced fracture of the left sacral ala. Despite these injuries, the sacroiliac joints remain congruous and the symphysis pubis is within normal limits for width. No active extravasation of intravenous contrast is seen within the pelvis to suggest active arterial bleeding. No large hemoperitoneum or expansive pelvic hematoma is identified.

The urinary bladder is partially filled and appears intact without wall thickening or perivesical fluid. Both kidneys, where visualized, show normal enhancement and excretion.

The prostate gland is normal in size for the patient's age. There is a small, benign-appearing simple cyst in the left kidney measuring approximately 1 cm. Additionally, there are mild degenerative changes noted at the L5-S1 facet joints. The visualized bowel loops are unremarkable without evidence of perforation or wall thickening. The visualized musculature of the pelvic girdle is symmetric.

**CONCLUSION:**
Multiple pelvic fractures involving the bilateral pubic rami and the left sacrum. No evidence of active bleeding or associated vascular complication.
