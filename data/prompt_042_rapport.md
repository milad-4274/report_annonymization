**INDICATION:** 71-year-old female presenting with right upper quadrant pain and elevated liver enzymes. Evaluate for bile duct obstruction or pancreatic mass.

**TECHNIQUE:** Computed tomography of the abdomen and pelvis was performed with intravenous contrast. Axial images were acquired and coronal reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver is normal in size and contour. However, there are multiple hypoattenuating lesions scattered throughout both the right and left lobes, the largest measuring approximately 2.4 cm in segment VII. These lesions are ill-defined and suspicious for malignancy. There is no evidence of a primary hepatic mass causing mass effect on adjacent structures. The gallbladder is present and unremarkable without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The intrahepatic and extrahepatic biliary ducts are not dilated; there is no evidence of choledocholithiasis or obstructive biliary pathology.

The pancreas demonstrates a normal lobulated morphology. There is no focal mass, cystic lesion, or evidence of pancreatitis. The pancreatic duct is not dilated.

The spleen and adrenal glands are within normal limits for age. The kidneys show symmetric enhancement without hydronephrosis or nephrolithiasis. A small amount of simple cortical cysts are noted in the left kidney, which are benign in appearance.

The gastrointestinal tract is non-dilated. There is no evidence of bowel wall thickening. Small amounts of diverticula are noted in the sigmoid colon without signs of acute diverticulitis. No free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites is identified.

The visualized pelvic organs are unremarkable for the patient's age. There is mild degenerative change noted in the lumbar spine and sacroiliac joints. The abdominal aorta is mildly ectatic but does not meet the criteria for aneurysm.

**CONCLUSION:**
Multiple suspicious hepatic lesions are present without an identifiable primary lesion. There is no evidence of bile duct obstruction.
