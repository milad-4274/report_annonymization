**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 83-year-old male presenting with slight diffuse abdominal pain and unintentional weight loss. Evaluate for bowel obstruction or other pathology.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**Findings:**
The lungs bases are clear without pleural effusion or consolidation. The heart size is within normal limits for age, though there is mild atherosclerotic calcification noted along the thoracic aorta.

The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous attenuation; no focal masses or intrahepatic biliary ductal dilatation is seen. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance and morphology. The adrenal glands are bilateral and symmetric.

There is an extensive, confluent soft tissue mass involving the retroperitoneum. This mass encases the infrarenal aorta and the inferior vena cava, extending from the level of the renal veins down into the pelvic brim. The mass appears relatively homogeneous and is highly suggestive of lymphoproliferative disease. Despite the bulk of the retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy, the ureters remain patent without evidence of hydronephrosis.

Regarding the bowel, there is no evidence of mechanical bowel obstruction. The small bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall thickness. The colon is unremarkable throughout its course. There is a small amount of diverticulosis noted in the sigmoid colon without signs of acute diverticulitis.

The urinary bladder is moderately distended with smooth walls. The prostate is enlarged, consistent with benign prostatic hyperplasia. No free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites is identified. The visualized osseous structures show mild degenerative changes of the lumbar spine and osteophytic spurring at the L4-L5 level.

**Conclusion:**
Extensive retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy suspicious for lymphoproliferative disease. No evidence of bowel obstruction or obstructive uropathy.
