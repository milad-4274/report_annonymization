**CT CHEST, ABDOMEN, AND PELVIS WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 88-year-old female presenting with recurrent constipation and unintentional weight loss. Evaluate for colorectal malignancy.

**Technique:** Helical CT imaging was performed from the thoracic apex through the symphysis pubis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Axial images were reconstructed. Coronal and sagittal reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are clear without evidence of focal consolidation, pleural effusion, or pneumothorax. There is mild, patchy atelectasis in the bilateral lung bases, which is common for the patient's age and positioning. The heart size is within normal limits, though there is some mild calcification noted along the coronary arteries and the aortic arch. The mediastinal contours are stable and no hilar lymphadenopathy is seen.

Moving to the abdomen, the liver demonstrates a smooth contour and normal attenuation without focal mass lesions. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in size and enhancement pattern. The adrenal glands are normal.

Within the retroperitoneum, there are multiple enlarged lymph nodes involving the para-aortic and inter-aortic stations. These nodes are bulky and demonstrate central low attenuation consistent with necrosis. This lymphadenopathy is extensive, causing some displacement of the adjacent vasculature.

The kidneys show symmetric excretion of contrast; however, there is a small, non-obstructing 4mm calculus in the lower pole of the right kidney. The urinary bladder is partially filled and the walls are thin.

Regarding the gastrointestinal tract, the stomach and small bowel loops are non-dilated. The colon is visualized throughout; while there is some prominence of the haustral folds, there is no evidence of a discrete obstructing mass or abnormal wall thickening to suggest a primary colorectal malignancy. There is a small amount of diverticulosis noted in the sigmoid colon without evidence of acute diverticulitis.

The pelvic organs are unremarkable for the patient's age. The bony structures show diffuse osteopenia and mild degenerative changes of the lumbar spine.

**Conclusion:**
Multiple large necrotic retroperitoneal lymph nodes are present. There are no associated complications such as hydronephrosis or vascular occlusion.
