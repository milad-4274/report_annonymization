**CT NECK WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 69-year-old female with history of T4 N1 glottic squamous cell carcinoma treated with chemoradiation two years ago. Evaluate for oncological recurrence.

**Technique:** Axial MDCT images were acquired from the skull base to the thoracic inlet following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were performed.

**Findings:**
The visualized portions of the skull base and cervical spine show mild degenerative changes, specifically some marginal osteophytes at C5-C6 and C6-C7, which is expected for the patient's age. The thyroid gland is normal in size and attenuation without focal nodules. The salivary glands, including the parotid and submandibular glands, demonstrate symmetric enhancement and no obstructive calculi.

Regarding the larynx, there is residual thickening of the glottic soft tissues consistent with post-radiation changes, but no new discrete mass or obstructive lesion is identified within the airway. The hypopharynx and nasopharynx are clear.

In the neck, there is a newly appeared enlarged lymph node in level III on the right side. This node demonstrates central low attenuation consistent with necrosis and shows peripheral rim enhancement. There is associated thinning of the overlying skin and subcutaneous fat, suggesting an inflammatory process or early cutaneous involvement. Other cervical lymph node stations, including levels I, II, IV, and V, do not show significant enlargement or abnormal morphology.

The major vascular structures, including the carotid arteries and internal jugular veins, are patent and maintain a normal course. The visualized lung apices are clear.

**Conclusion:**
Newly developed necrotic lymphadenopathy in right level III with associated overlying skin thinning, highly suggestive of oncological recurrence.
