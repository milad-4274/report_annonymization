**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 39-year-old male presenting with a median abdominal wall bulge five months after hysterectomy. Evaluate for hematoma.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the symphysis pubis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**Findings:**
The lungs bases are clear without pleural effusion or pneumothorax. The heart size is within normal limits and there is no pericardial effusion.

The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous attenuation; no focal masses are identified. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or radiopaque stones. The biliary tree is not dilated. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. The adrenal glands are symmetric and normal in size.

There is a small, non-obstructing 3 mm calculus in the right lower pole of the kidney. Both kidneys demonstrate symmetric enhancement and excretion. There is no hydronephrosis.

The gastrointestinal tract is unremarkable. There is no evidence of bowel wall thickening or obstruction. No free intraperitoneal air or fluid is seen.

Within the anterior abdominal wall, centered in the suprapubic region, there is a well-defined solid soft tissue lesion measuring approximately 3 cm in greatest dimension. This mass is located within the subcutaneous tissues and musculature of the midline. It shows moderate enhancement following contrast administration. There is no evidence of an acute or organizing hematoma, nor is there a fluid collection suggestive of a seroma. Given the clinical history of prior surgery, the differential includes a desmoid tumor or potentially endometriosis, although the latter is noted given the surgical context provided.

The urinary bladder is moderately distended with smooth walls. The pelvic bones are intact. There is a small amount of benign degenerative change noted at the L4-L5 facet joints.

**Conclusion:**
Solid 3 cm enhancing supra-pubic abdominal wall lesion. Findings are suspicious for a desmoid tumor or endometriosis; no evidence of hematoma.
