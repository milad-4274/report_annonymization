**INDICATION:** 59-year-old male with newly diagnosed Child-Pugh Class B cirrhosis and recent history of variceal bleeding. Evaluate for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and portal vein thrombosis.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar, multisequence imaging of the abdomen was performed before and after the administration of intravenous gadolinium contrast. Dynamic post-contrast series were obtained during the arterial, portal venous, and delayed phases.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates a nodular surface contour and heterogeneous parenchymal signal intensity consistent with the provided history of cirrhosis. There are multiple focal lesions located within the left hepatic lobe, the largest measuring approximately 2.4 cm in the segment IVa region. These lesions demonstrate classic arterial phase hyperenhancement with subsequent washout on the portal venous and delayed phases, highly suggestive of hepatocellular carcinoma. The right hepatic lobe is relatively spared, though there are several small, non-enhancing cysts noted in segment VII, which are benign in appearance.

The portal vein is patent throughout its course. There is no evidence of thrombus within the main portal vein, the splenic vein, or the superior mesenteric vein. However, there is prominent collaterals noted around the splenic hilum and in the perigastric region, consistent with portal hypertension. The hepatic veins are patent.

The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid; no gallstones are identified. The common bile duct is within normal limits for caliber.

The spleen is enlarged, measuring 14 cm in craniocaudal dimension. The pancreas appears unremarkable in morphology and signal intensity, with no focal masses or ductal dilatation.

The adrenal glands are bilateral and symmetric without nodules. The kidneys show normal cortical thickness and excretion of contrast, although there is a small, simple-appearing cyst in the lower pole of the left kidney. No hydronephrosis is seen.

There is a moderate amount of free fluid surrounding the liver and spleen, consistent with ascites. No lymphadenopathy is identified in the retroperitoneum or mesenteric regions.

**CONCLUSION:**
Multiple lesions in the left hepatic lobe demonstrating characteristic enhancement patterns suggestive of hepatocellular carcinoma. No evidence of portal vein thrombosis.
