**INDICATION:** 68-year-old female with Child-Pugh Class B cirrhosis. Screening for hepatocellular carcinoma.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar, multisequence imaging of the abdomen was performed before and after the administration of intravenous gadoxetate disodium. Dynamic post-contrast series were obtained during the arterial, portal venous, and delayed phases.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates a nodular surface contour and heterogeneous parenchymal signal intensity consistent with the known history of cirrhosis. There is a focal mass identified within segment 3 measuring 29 mm in maximal diameter. This lesion exhibits intense non-rim arterial phase hyperenhancement with subsequent portal venous and delayed phase washout. The lesion appears hypointense on the hepatobiliary phase. These features are characteristic of LI-RADS 5. No other definite enhancing nodules are seen throughout the remainder of the hepatic parenchyma.

The gallbladder is present and unremarkable without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The intrahepatic and extrahepatic biliary ducts are not dilated. The portal vein is patent with normal direction of flow; however, there is mild prominence of the splenic vein. The spleen is mildly enlarged, measuring 14 cm in craniocaudal dimension.

The pancreas is normal in morphology and signal intensity without focal masses or ductal dilatation. The adrenal glands are bilateral and appear symmetric and unremarkable. Both kidneys demonstrate normal cortical thickness and excretion, though there is a small, simple-appearing cyst in the lower pole of the left kidney measuring 8 mm.

There is a small amount of free fluid noted within the perihepatic space, consistent with mild ascites. No lymphadenopathy is identified in the retroperitoneum or porta hepatis. The visualized portions of the bowel are unremarkable.

**CONCLUSION:**
A 29 mm lesion in segment 3 of the liver meeting LI-RADS 5 criteria for hepatocellular carcinoma. There is associated mild ascites.
