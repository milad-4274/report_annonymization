**INDICATION:** 58-year-old male, active smoker for over 30 years, presents with chronic cough. Evaluate for malignancy, COPD, or infectious process.

**TECHNIQUE:** Helical CT acquisition of the chest was performed without intravenous contrast. Volumetric data were obtained and reconstructed in axial and coronal planes.

**FINDINGS:**
The lungs are clear of pleural effusion or pneumothorax. There is a 24 mm solid, peripherally located nodule within the right lower lobe, demonstrating slightly irregular margins. The remaining lung parenchyma shows no evidence of consolidation or suspicious masses. There are mild centrilobular emphysematous changes noted in the upper lobes, consistent with the patient's smoking history. No significant bronchiectasis is seen.

The mediastinum and hilar regions demonstrate no lymphadenopathy; the lymph nodes are within normal size limits. The heart size is normal and there is no pericardial effusion. The thoracic aorta and great vessels follow a normal course and caliber.

The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and adrenal glands, appear unremarkable. There is a small, benign-appearing hepatic cyst measuring 1 cm in the right lobe of the liver. The bony structures of the thoracic cage show mild degenerative changes of the thoracic spine, which is expected for the patient's age. Soft tissues of the chest wall are intact.

**CONCLUSION:**
There is a 24 mm peripheral nodule in the right lower lobe. No associated pleural effusion or mediastinal lymphadenopathy is identified.
