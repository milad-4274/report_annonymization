**CT CHEST WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 75-year-old female. Follow up one year after operated left ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma. Evaluate for oncological recurrence.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the thoracic inlet through the upper abdomen following the administration of intravenous contrast. Coronal and sagittal reformations were performed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are clear without any focal consolidation, suspicious pulmonary nodules, or pleural effusions. There is no evidence of pneumothorax. The tracheobronchial tree is patent and unremarkable in caliber.

Regarding the mediastinum, there is a noted increase in size of a previously identified lymph node located in the right cardiophrenic angle, which now measures 12 mm in short axis. The remaining mediastinal, hilar, and axillary lymph nodes are within normal limits for size and morphology. The heart size is normal, and there is no pericardial effusion. The thoracic aorta and its primary branches show mild atherosclerotic calcifications, which is expected for the patient's age.

The visualized portions of the upper abdomen are unremarkable. Specifically, the liver and spleen are normal in appearance. There is some mild degenerative change noted in the thoracic spine and sternoclavicular joints. The chest wall and visualized soft tissues are unremarkable.

**Conclusion:**
Size augmentation of a right cardiophrenic angle lymph node, now measuring 12 mm, concerning for oncological recurrence. No associated pleural effusion or pulmonary nodules identified.
