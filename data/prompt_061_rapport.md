**INDICATION:** 57-year-old male with chronic cough. Evaluate for lung cancer versus infection.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT images were acquired through the chest without intravenous contrast. Coronal and sagittal reformations were performed.

**FINDINGS:**
The lungs are clear overall without evidence of large pleural effusions or pneumothorax. There is a small, well-defined 9 mm endobronchial nodule noted within the right lower lobe bronchus, causing some mild distal narrowing of the airway lumen. No associated obstructive pneumonia or significant collapse of the distal lung parenchyma is seen. The remaining bronchial tree appears patent.

The pulmonary arteries and veins are normal in caliber. The heart size is within normal limits and there is no pericardial effusion. The thoracic aorta is mildly ectatic but does not show aneurysmal dilation.

The mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes are not enlarged by CT criteria. The esophagus is unremarkable. There is a small amount of degenerative change noted in the thoracic spine, specifically mild osteophytic spurring at T6 and T7. A few small, benign-appearing calcified granulomas are noted in the left upper lobe. The visualized portions of the upper abdomen, including the liver and adrenal glands, are unremarkable.

**CONCLUSION:**
There is a 9 mm endobronchial nodule in the right lower lobe. No associated obstructive atelectasis or post-obstructive pneumonia is identified.
