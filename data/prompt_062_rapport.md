**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 71-year-old female presenting with right upper quadrant pain and abnormal liver enzymes. Evaluate for bile duct obstruction or pancreatic mass.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**Findings:**
The liver demonstrates a heterogeneous appearance. There are multiple hypoattenuating focal lesions scattered throughout both lobes, the largest measuring approximately 2.4 cm in segment VII, which are highly suspicious for metastatic disease. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. There is no evidence of intrahepatic or extrahepatic biliary ductal dilatation. The common bile duct is within normal limits for the patient's age.

The pancreas is normal in morphology and attenuation. There is no evidence of a pancreatic mass, focal lesion, or inflammatory changes. The pancreatic duct is not dilated.

The spleen and adrenal glands are unremarkable in size and enhancement.

Within the abdomen, there is a circumferential, irregularly enhancing soft tissue mass involving the ascending colon. This lesion does not cause high-grade stenosis of the bowel lumen at this time but appears suspicious for primary malignancy. The remaining small and large bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall thickness.

There is a small amount of simple free fluid in the pelvis. A few small, non-specific calcifications are noted within the pelvic vasculature. The urinary bladder is partially filled and unremarkable. The visualized kidneys show symmetric excretion of contrast without hydronephrosis.

The visualized lower lungs are clear. No significant lymphadenopathy is identified in the retroperitoneum or mesenteric regions.

**Conclusion:**
Suspicious non-stenosing right colonic lesion with associated multiple suspicious hepatic lesions. No evidence of bile duct obstruction or pancreatic mass.
