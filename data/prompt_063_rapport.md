**INDICATION:** 83-year-old male presenting with slight diffuse abdominal pain and unintentional weight loss. Evaluate for bowel obstruction or other intra-abdominal pathology.

**TECHNIQUE:** Computed tomography of the abdomen and pelvis was performed with intravenous contrast administration. Axial images were acquired and reconstructed in coronal and sagittal planes.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour without focal mass lesions or intrahepatic biliary ductal dilatation. The gallbladder is present and unremarkable. The spleen and pancreas are normal in appearance, with no evidence of inflammation or cystic lesions.

Regarding the gastrointestinal tract, there is no evidence of high-grade bowel obstruction. The small bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall thickness throughout. The colon is unremarkable without evidence of diverticulitis or wall thickening. The appendix is visualized and appears normal.

In the retroperitoneum, there is an abnormal soft tissue density involving the periaortic and pericaval regions. This plaque-like mass encases the infrarenal aorta and the inferior vena cava, extending inferiorly toward the pelvic brim. There is a characteristic medial displacement of the ureters, which appear mildly stretched, though no significant proximal hydroureteronephrosis is noted at this time. The adrenal glands are symmetric and normal in size.

The kidneys show a mild decrease in cortical volume consistent with age, but otherwise maintain normal enhancement and morphology. There are a few small, simple cortical cysts bilaterally. A small amount of atherosclerotic calcification is noted within the abdominal aortic wall.

The urinary bladder is partially filled and has smooth walls. No free fluid or free intraperitoneal air is identified. The visualized lung bases are clear. The bony structures of the spine and pelvis show mild degenerative changes and osteophytic spurring, which are expected for the patient's age.

**CONCLUSION:**
Radiological findings are highly suggestive of retroperitoneal fibrosis. There is no evidence of associated obstructive uropathy.
