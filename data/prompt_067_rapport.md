**CT CHEST, ABDOMEN, AND PELVIS WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 88-year-old male presenting with recurrent constipation and weight loss. Evaluate for colorectal malignancy.

**Technique:** Helical CT acquisition was performed from the thoracic apex through the symphysis pubis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are clear without evidence of focal consolidation, pleural effusion, or pneumothorax. There is mild, chronic centrilobular emphysema noted in the upper lobes, which is expected for the patient's age. The heart size is within normal limits, though there is mild atherosclerotic calcification seen along the coronary arteries and the aortic arch. The mediastinal and hilar contours are unremarkable with no significant lymphadenopathy identified in the chest.

Moving to the abdomen, the liver demonstrates normal size and attenuation without focal mass lesions. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. Both kidneys show symmetric enhancement and excretion; however, there are small, non-obstructing renal cortical cysts bilaterally. The adrenal glands are normal.

Regarding the gastrointestinal tract, the stomach and small bowel loops are non-dilated. The colon is unremarkable throughout its course, showing no evidence of wall thickening, apple-core lesions, or obstructive patterns to suggest primary colorectal malignancy.

In the pelvis, there is irregular, asymmetric thickening of the prostate gland. Multiple enlarged lymph nodes are visualized within the internal and external iliac chains, consistent with pelvic lymphadenopathy. Additionally, several punched-out lytic lesions are noted within the pelvic girdle and the proximal femurs. The urinary bladder is unremarkable. No free fluid or free air is seen within the peritoneal cavity.

**Conclusion:**
Irregular prostate gland thickening associated with pelvic lymphadenopathy and multiple lytic bone lesions. No evidence of colorectal malignancy.
