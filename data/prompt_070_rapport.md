**INDICATION:** 69-year-old female presenting with fever and difficulty swallowing. Elevated inflammatory markers. Evaluate for cervical abscess.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the neck was performed from the skull base to the thoracic inlet following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The visualized portions of the skull base and cervicofacial bones demonstrate no acute fracture or aggressive osseous lesion. There is mild degenerative change noted at the C4-C5 and C5-C6 disc spaces with associated small marginal osteophytes, which is expected for the patient's age.

Regarding the soft tissues, there is a well-defined fluid collection located within the left retropharyngeal space, extending from the level of the C2 vertebral body down to C4. This collection demonstrates peripheral rim enhancement and internal low attenuation consistent with an abscess. There is associated fat stranding and thickening of the retropharyngeal fascia. The airway remains patent, although there is mild rightward displacement of the larynx due to the mass effect from the left-sided collection.

Multiple enlarged, rounded lymph nodes are noted bilaterally in the cervical chains, most prominent on the left side in levels II and III, appearing reactive in nature given the surrounding inflammatory changes.

The thyroid gland is normal in size and attenuation without focal nodules. The salivary glands, including the parotids and submandibular glands, are symmetric and unremarkable. The visualized vasculature, including the carotid arteries and internal jugular veins, are patent without evidence of thrombosis. The visualized lung apices are clear.

**CONCLUSION:**
Left retropharyngeal abscess with associated reactive cervical lymphadenopathy. No evidence of airway compromise or extension into the mediastinum.
