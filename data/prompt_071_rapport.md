**INDICATION:** 49-year-old male presenting with a palpable median abdominal wall bulge. Evaluate for possible hematoma.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging was performed through the abdomen and pelvis. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The imaged abdominal wall demonstrates a well-circumscribed, oval-shaped mass located in the subcutaneous fat anterior to the rectus abdominis muscle. This lesion measures approximately 4 cm in its greatest dimension. It exhibits uniform low attenuation consistent with macroscopic fat. There is no internal nodularity, no thick septations, and no evidence of deep extension into the muscular fascia or peritoneal cavity. No surrounding inflammatory stranding or fluid collection is noted to suggest an acute hematoma.

The liver is normal in size and contour without focal lesions. The gallbladder is unremarkable. The spleen and pancreas are within normal limits for age. Both kidneys show normal enhancement and excretion without hydronephrosis. The adrenal glands are unremarkable.

The stomach and small bowel loops are non-dilated. The colon is unremarkable throughout. There is a small amount of benign physiological fluid in the pelvis. A few scattered, non-specific small hepatic cysts are noted, which are common incidental findings. There is mild degenerative change seen at the L3-L4 facet joints. No lymphadenopathy is identified in the retroperitoneum or pelvic regions. The visualized lungs at the bases are clear.

**CONCLUSION:**
There is a 4 cm uncomplicated subcutaneous lipoma anterior to the rectus abdominis. No features of nodularity or deep extension are present, and there is no evidence of hematoma.
