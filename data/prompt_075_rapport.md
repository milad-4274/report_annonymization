**INDICATION:** 59-year-old male with newly diagnosed Child-Pugh Class B cirrhosis. Ultrasound demonstrated two echogenic liver lesions. Evaluate for hepatocellular carcinoma versus other etiologies.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar, multisequence imaging of the abdomen was performed before and after the administration of intravenous gadolinium contrast. Dynamic post-contrast series were obtained during the arterial, portal venous, and delayed phases.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates a nodular surface contour and a coarse parenchymal echo pattern throughout, consistent with the known history of cirrhosis. There is mild enlargement of the left lobe and slight atrophy of the caudate lobe. Within the right lobe of the liver, there are two well-circumscribed lesions. The first is located in segment VII measuring 2.4 cm, and the second is in segment VIII measuring 1.8 cm. Both lesions demonstrate intense, homogeneous enhancement on the arterial phase with characteristic peripheral globular puddling and progressive centripetal fill-in on the portal venous and delayed images. No evidence of washout or capsule enhancement is seen. These features are classic for hepatic hemangiomas. No other focal masses or suspicious nodules are identified within the liver parenchyma.

The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid; no gallstones are visualized. The intrahepatic and extrahepatic biliary ducts are not dilated. The portal vein is patent, although it shows a slightly increased caliber, and the hepatic veins are patent.

The spleen is mildly enlarged, measuring 14 cm in craniocaudal dimension. There is a small amount of free fluid noted in the perihepatic space and around the spleen, consistent with mild ascites.

The pancreas is unremarkable in morphology and signal intensity, with no focal lesions or ductal dilatation. The adrenal glands are bilateral and appear normal in size and configuration. The kidneys show normal cortical thickness and excretion of contrast without hydronephrosis. The visualized portions of the bowel loops are non-dilated and show no wall thickening.

**CONCLUSION:**
Two typical hepatic hemangiomas. No evidence of hepatocellular carcinoma.
