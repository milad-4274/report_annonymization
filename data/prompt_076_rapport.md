**INDICATION:** 64-year-old female with chronic abdominal pain. Ultrasound demonstrated a 5 cm subcapsular mass in the right hepatic lobe. Evaluation for nature of the lesion.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar, multisequence magnetic resonance imaging of the abdomen was performed before and after the administration of intravenous gadolinium contrast. Dynamic post-contrast imaging was obtained during the arterial, portal venous, and delayed phases.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and smooth surface contours. There is a well-circumscribed, focal mass located subcapsularly within the right hepatic lobe measuring approximately 5.2 x 4.8 cm. The lesion is predominantly isointense to slightly hypointense on T1-weighted images and mildly hyperintense on T2-weighted sequences. Following administration of intravenous contrast, there is intense, homogeneous enhancement during the arterial phase, becoming nearly isointense to the surrounding hepatic parenchyma on portal venous and delayed phases. A characteristic central stellate scar is noted, which remains hypointense on T1 and shows delayed enhancement. No peripheral capsule or washout is identified.

The gallbladder is present and unremarkable without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The intrahepatic and extrahepatic biliary ducts are not dilated. The portal vein and hepatic veins are patent with normal flow voids.

The spleen is normal in size and signal intensity. The pancreas demonstrates a normal lobulated contour and signal throughout the head, body, and tail; no focal masses or ductal dilatation observed. The adrenal glands are symmetric and unremarkable.

Within the abdomen, there is a small amount of simple free fluid in the pelvis, likely physiologic. A few small, benign-appearing hepatic cysts are noted in the left lobe, the largest measuring 8 mm. No lymphadenopathy is seen in the porta hepatis or retroperitoneum. The visualized portions of the kidneys show normal cortical thickness and no hydronephrosis.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are characteristic of focal nodular hyperplasia (FNH) involving the right hepatic lobe. There are no features suggestive of malignancy or associated complications.
