**CT CHEST WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 75-year-old female with history of operated left ovarian tumor three years ago. Evaluation for oncological recurrence.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the thoracic inlet through the upper abdomen following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were performed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are clear without any focal consolidation, suspicious pulmonary nodules, or masses. There is no evidence of pleural effusion or pneumothorax. The tracheobronchial tree is patent and unremarkable.

Regarding the mediastinum, the heart size is within normal limits for the patient's age. The thoracic aorta and its primary branches show mild atherosclerotic calcifications, which is an expected finding. No significant lymphadenopathy is noted in the hilar or mediastinal stations. The esophagus is normal in caliber.

There is a notable flattening of the left hemi-diaphragm compared to the right side, which is suggestive of left-sided diaphragmatic palsy. The visualized portions of the upper abdomen show a small amount of hepatic steatosis, but the liver morphology is otherwise normal. There are some mild degenerative changes noted in the thoracic spine and visualized AC joints. No abdominal ascites or suspicious peritoneal nodules are seen in the limited field of view.

**Conclusion:**
Flattening of the left hemi-diaphragm consistent with diaphragmatic palsy; no associated pleural effusion or acute pulmonary complication identified.
