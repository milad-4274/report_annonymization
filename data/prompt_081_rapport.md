**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 54-year-old male presenting with right upper quadrant pain, fever, and elevated CRP. Evaluate for cholecystitis.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**Findings:**
The liver is normal in size and contour. There is a large, peripherally enhancing fluid collection measuring approximately 8 cm in the right lobe of the liver, consistent with a hepatic abscess. The internal contents show low attenuation with some internal debris. There is mild inflammatory stranding in the adjacent hepatic parenchyma. The gallbladder is present and normally distended without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid; no radiopaque gallstones are identified. The intrahepatic and extrahepatic biliary ducts are not dilated.

The spleen, pancreas, and adrenal glands are unremarkable in appearance. The kidneys demonstrate symmetric enhancement and excretion without evidence of hydronephrosis or nephrolithiasis. A small, non-obstructing 3 mm renal cyst is noted in the lower pole of the left kidney.

The gastrointestinal tract is normal in caliber. There is no evidence of bowel wall thickening or obstruction. A few small, non-specific diverticula are noted in the sigmoid colon. No free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites is seen.

The abdominal aorta is normal in diameter without aneurysmal dilation. The visualized portions of the lungs at the bases are clear. No significant lymphadenopathy is identified within the retroperitoneum or pelvic regions. The urinary bladder is unremarkable. The visualized bony structures are intact, though there is mild degenerative change noted in the lumbar spine.

**Conclusion:**
Large 8 cm abscess in the right lobe of the liver. No evidence of gallbladder wall thickening or cholecystitis. No associated perforation or gross abscess rupture into the peritoneum.
