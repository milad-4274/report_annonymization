**EXAMINATION:** CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast

**INDICATION:** 78-year-old female presenting with left flank pain and slight leukocytosis. Evaluate for urinary stones or pyelonephritis.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the symphysis pubis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and attenuation without focal mass or intrahepatic biliary ductal dilatation. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. The adrenal glands are symmetric and normal in configuration.

Regarding the gastrointestinal tract, there is no evidence of bowel wall thickening or obstruction. A small amount of diverticulosis is noted within the sigmoid colon without signs of acute diverticulitis. There is mild atherosclerotic calcification seen along the abdominal aorta.

The kidneys are normal in size and position. There is no evidence of hydronephrosis or radiopaque calculi within the collecting systems. No perinephric stranding or renal cortical thinning is observed to suggest acute pyelonephritis. The urinary bladder is partially filled and appears smooth-walled.

Within the retroperitoneum, there is a large, peripherally enhancing fluid collection located within the left psoas muscle measuring approximately 10 cm in maximal craniocaudal dimension. This collection demonstrates internal low attenuation consistent with an abscess, with associated inflammatory fat stranding extending into the adjacent retroperitoneal space. The right psoas muscle is normal in caliber and attenuation.

The visualized lung bases are clear. No pleural effusions are identified. The bony structures of the pelvis and lumbar spine show mild degenerative changes and osteophytic spurring, which is expected for the patient's age.

**CONCLUSION:**
Large 10 cm abscess involving the left psoas muscle. No evidence of associated pneumoperitoneum or extension into the peritoneal cavity.
