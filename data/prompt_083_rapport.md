**RADIOLOGY REPORT**

**Indication:** 47-year-old male presenting with lower back pain, right greater than left, associated with fever. Clinical question asks to rule out pyelonephritis.

**Technique:** Computed Tomography (CT) of the abdomen and pelvis was performed. Axial images were acquired without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The kidneys are normal in size and position. There is no evidence of hydronephrosis, nephrolithiasis, or perinephric stranding. The renal contours are smooth, and there are no focal cortical defects or masses to suggest pyelonephritis. The adrenal glands are unremarkable.

The liver demonstrates normal attenuation and smooth margins; no focal lesions are identified. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or radiopaque stones. The spleen and pancreas are within normal limits for size and morphology. The visualized bowel loops are non-dilated and show no evidence of wall thickening or obstruction. There is no free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites.

Regarding the musculoskeletal system, there is an area of decreased bone density and irregular endplate erosion involving the L2 and L3 vertebral bodies, centered around the intervening intervertebral disc space. This appearance is highly suggestive of infectious spondylodiscitis. However, the vertebral heights are well-maintained, and there is no evidence of vertebral collapse or acute compression fracture at this level. The remaining lumbar vertebrae demonstrate normal alignment. There is mild marginal osteophyte formation at L4-L5, consistent with age-related degenerative changes. A small, benign-appearing osseous island is noted in the right iliac wing. The visualized pelvic girdle is otherwise intact.

The bladder is partially filled and appears unremarkable. The pelvic lymph nodes are not enlarged.

**Conclusion:**
Findings are consistent with infectious spondylarthritis of the L2-L3 disc. There are no signs of vertebral collapse or associated spinal instability.
