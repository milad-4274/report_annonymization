**INDICATION:** 55-year-old female presenting with acute upper abdominal pain and elevated serum lipase levels. Evaluate for pancreatitis and potential complications.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the abdomen and pelvis was performed following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous parenchymal attenuation; no focal masses or intrahepatic biliary dilatation is noted. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid, although a few small, nonobstructing choleliths are seen. The spleen and adrenal glands are unremarkable in appearance.

The pancreas is diffusely edematous and enlarged with ill-defined margins. There is a large, non-enhancing fluid collection measuring approximately 8 cm in its greatest dimension located within the pancreatic parenchyma and extending into the peripancreatic space, consistent with walled-off necrosis. Stranding is noted throughout the peripancreatic fat. The pancreatic duct is not significantly dilated.

The kidneys are normal in size and position with symmetric enhancement and no evidence of hydronephrosis. A small, simple cortical cyst is noted in the lower pole of the left kidney, which is an incidental finding. The urinary bladder is moderately distended with smooth walls.

The gastrointestinal tract is unremarkable, though there is mild reactive thickening of the duodenum adjacent to the pancreatic head. No free intraperitoneal air or high-attenuation fluid is identified in the paracolic gutters or the pelvis. The visualized aorta and major vascular structures are patent and of normal caliber for the patient's age. Visualized lung bases are clear.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are consistent with acute pancreatitis complicated by an 8 cm necrotic collection.
