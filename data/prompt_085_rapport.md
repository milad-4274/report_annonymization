**CT CHEST WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 68-year-old male presenting with fever and difficulty breathing. Evaluate for pneumonia.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired through the chest from the thoracic inlet to the adrenal glands following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were performed.

**Findings:**
The visualized portions of the neck and thoracic inlet are unremarkable. The trachea is midline and patent. 

Regarding the lungs, there is a large, complex pleural collection within the left hemithorax. This collection is multi-loculated, demonstrating peripheral enhancing walls and internal septations, consistent with an empyema. There is associated compressive atelectasis of the adjacent left lower lobe. The right lung is clear without focal consolidation, suspicious nodules, or pleural effusion. No pneumothorax is identified on either side.

The mediastinal and hilar contours are within normal limits. The heart size is mildly enlarged, though no pericardial effusion is seen. There is no evidence of mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The great vessels, including the aorta and pulmonary arteries, show normal caliber and course.

The bony structures of the thoracic cage demonstrate mild degenerative changes of the thoracic spine and some patchy osteopenia, which is expected for the patient's age. The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and spleen, appear unremarkable within the limits of this study.

**Conclusion:**
Multi-loculated left empyema with associated compressive atelectasis of the left lower lobe. No evidence of pneumothorax.
