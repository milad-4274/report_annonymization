**INDICATION:** 77-year-old female presenting with fever 10 days status post right total hip arthroplasty. Evaluate for infection or abscess.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT images were acquired through the pelvis without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**FINDINGS:**
The visualized pelvic organs are unremarkable for age. The urinary bladder is partially filled and demonstrates smooth wall thickness without obvious intraluminal masses. There is a small amount of free fluid within the pouch of Douglas, which is likely physiologic. The sigmoid colon shows mild diverticulosis without evidence of acute wall thickening or pericolic stranding.

Regarding the musculoskeletal system, there is hardware in place from a recent right total hip arthroplasty. The prosthetic components are well-positioned. However, there is a localized, peripherally enhancing fluid collection measuring approximately 3.2 x 2.1 cm located along the lateral aspect of the right hip, corresponding to the previous surgical approach. This collection is associated with mild surrounding soft tissue edema and fat stranding. No gas bubbles are seen within the collection.

The left hip joint appears intact without effusion or dislocation. The visualized pelvic girdle bones show mild degenerative changes and scattered osteopenia, consistent with the patient's age. No suspicious blastic or lytic lesions are identified in the visualized pelvic bones. The visualized lower abdominal aorta is mildly ectatic but without aneurysm.

**CONCLUSION:**
Fluid collection along the surgical approach of the right hip arthroplasty, consistent with an infected collection or abscess. No evidence of deep periprosthetic joint space extension.
