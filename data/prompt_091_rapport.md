**INDICATION:** 62-year-old male, 5 days status post right inguinal fossa kidney transplant. Presents with right lower quadrant pain. Evaluate for complication, specifically abscess versus lymphocele.

**TECHNIQUE:** Computed tomography of the abdomen and pelvis was performed with intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**

**Abdomen:** The liver demonstrates normal size and attenuation without focal lesions. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. The adrenal glands are symmetric and normal in morphology. There is no evidence of free intraperitoneal air or high-attenuation fluid within the peritoneal cavity.

**Kidneys and Ureters:** The native kidneys are visualized and appear unremarkable for the patient's age. The transplanted kidney is located in the right inguinal fossa. It demonstrates appropriate enhancement and perfusion. There is no evidence of cortical necrosis or urinary tract obstruction. The ureter is patent.

**Pelvis and Transplant Site:** In the region surrounding the transplanted kidney, there is a large, peripherally enhancing fluid collection measuring approximately 6.4 x 4.2 cm. This collection exhibits an irregular, thick wall with internal septations and contains scattered gas bubbles, consistent with an infected lymphocele. There is mild inflammatory stranding of the adjacent pelvic fat. The urinary bladder is midline and unremarkable.

**Additional Findings:** There is mild degenerative change noted at the L4-L5 and L5-S1 disc spaces with small marginal osteophytes. A small, 4mm non-obstructing renal cyst is noted in the left native kidney. The visualized bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall thickness.

**CONCLUSION:** Large infected lymphocele associated with the right inguinal fossa kidney transplant. No evidence of vascular complication or graft thrombosis.
