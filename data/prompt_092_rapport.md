**RADIOLOGY REPORT**

**Indication:** 54-year-old female, 8 days status post left hemicolectomy. Presenting with persisting high inflammatory markers. Evaluate for complication or anastomotic insufficiency.

**Technique:** Contrast-enhanced CT scan of the abdomen and pelvis (AP) was performed. Axial images were acquired and reconstructed in coronal and sagittal planes. Oral and intravenous contrast were administered.

**Findings:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and attenuation without focal lesions; the gallbladder is present and unremarkable. The spleen, pancreas, and adrenal glands show no significant abnormalities. There is no evidence of free intraperitoneal air or high-attenuation fluid within the peritoneal cavity.

Regarding the gastrointestinal tract, there are postoperative changes consistent with a recent left hemicolectomy. The colorectal anastomosis is visualized; however, there is an associated peripherally enhancing fluid collection located in the presacral space, measuring approximately 3.2 x 2.1 cm. There is associated fat stranding in the surrounding pelvic connective tissue. No extraluminal gas is clearly identified immediately adjacent to the suture line, but the presacral collection is suspicious for a localized leak.

The urinary bladder is normally distended with smooth walls. Both kidneys demonstrate symmetric enhancement and excretion of contrast without hydronephrosis.

Incidental findings include a small, simple cortical cyst in the right kidney, measuring 1 cm, which is benign in appearance. There are mild degenerative changes noted in the L4-L5 facet joints. The pelvic bones are otherwise intact.

**Conclusion:**
Presacral fluid collection following colorectal anastomosis, highly suggestive of anastomotic insufficiency. No evidence of generalized pneumoperitoneum.
