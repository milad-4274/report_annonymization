**INDICATION:** 92-year-old female presenting with right upper quadrant pain, fever, and elevated inflammatory markers. Evaluate for cholecystitis versus colitis.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the abdomen and pelvis was performed following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour without focal mass lesions or intrahepatic biliary ductal dilatation. The gallbladder is significantly distended and exhibits thickened, edematous walls. There is prominent pericholecystic fluid and inflammatory stranding within the adjacent fat. A small, calcified gallstone is noted impacted within the gallbladder neck. The common bile duct is within normal limits for the patient's age.

The spleen, pancreas, and adrenal glands are unremarkable in appearance. The kidneys show symmetric enhancement without hydronephrosis or nephrolithiasis.

Regarding the bowel, there is no evidence of wall thickening, mucosal edema, or mesenteric stranding to suggest colitis. The appendix is visualized and appears normal. The bladder is partially filled and unremarkable.

Incidental findings include mild patchy atherosclerosis of the abdominal aorta and its primary branches. There is moderate osteophytic spurring and degenerative disc disease throughout the lumbar spine, which is expected for the patient's age. Small, benign-appearing hepatic cysts are noted in the left lobe.

No free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites is identified. The visualized lung bases are clear.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are consistent with acute cholecystitis. There is no evidence of gallbladder perforation or abscess formation.
