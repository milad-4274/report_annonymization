**INDICATION:** 39-year-old female presenting with fever and dyspnea. Question of occult pneumonia following an inconclusive chest X-ray.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the chest was performed without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The lungs are partially visualized due to the presence of fluid. There is a moderate to large right-sided pleural effusion. The pleural surfaces are notably thickened and there is evidence of pleural enhancement and "split pleura" sign, which is highly suggestive of an empyema. Associated compressive atelectasis is noted in the right lower lobe. The left lung is clear without focal consolidation, suspicious nodules, or masses. No pneumothorax is identified on either side.

The mediastinal contours are within normal limits. The heart size is normal and there is no pericardial effusion. The trachea and mainstem bronchi remain patent. No significant mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy is seen.

The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and spleen, appear unremarkable. There is a small, simple hepatic cyst measuring approximately 1 cm in the right lobe of the liver, which is an incidental finding. The adrenal glands are normal in morphology.

The bony structures of the thoracic cage are intact. There is a mild degenerative change noted at the T7-T8 facet joints. The visualized soft tissues of the chest wall are unremarkable.

**CONCLUSION:**
Right-sided pleural effusion with features consistent with empyema. No evidence of pneumothorax.
