**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 54-year-old male presenting with right upper quadrant pain, fever, and elevated CRP. Evaluate for cholecystitis.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**Findings:**
The liver is normal in size and contour. However, there is a large, unique hepatic mass identified within the right lobe that demonstrates atypical enhancement patterns; it cannot be fully characterized on the current study. The gallbladder is unremarkable without wall thickening, pericholecystic fluid, or radiopaque gallstones. The common bile duct is not dilated.

The spleen, pancreas, and adrenal glands are normal in appearance and show no focal lesions. The kidneys demonstrate symmetric enhancement and excretion without evidence of hydronephrosis or nephrolithiasis. There is a small, simple cortical cyst in the left kidney, measuring approximately 1 cm, which is an incidental finding.

The bowel loops are non-dilated and show no signs of obstruction or inflammatory wall thickening. There is a small amount of diverticulosis noted in the sigmoid colon without evidence of acute diverticulitis. No free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites is seen.

The abdominal aorta is normal in caliber. The pelvic organs, including the urinary bladder and prostate, are unremarkable for the patient's age. Visualized lung bases are clear. No significant lymphadenopathy is identified in the retroperitoneum or pelvis.

**Conclusion:**
Large, uncharacterized hepatic mass. No evidence of cholecystitis.
