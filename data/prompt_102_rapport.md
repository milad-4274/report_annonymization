**INDICATION:** 78-year-old female presenting with left flank pain. No biological markers of infection noted. Evaluate for urinary stones.

**TECHNIQUE:** Computed tomography of the abdomen and pelvis was performed. Axial images were acquired without intravenous contrast. Coronal and sagittal reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver is normal in size and contour, showing homogeneous attenuation without focal lesions. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid; no radiopaque gallstones are seen. The spleen and pancreas demonstrate unremarkable morphology and attenuation.

Regarding the urinary tract, both kidneys are normal in position and size. There is no evidence of hydronephrosis or perinephric stranding. No radiopaque calculi are identified within the renal collecting systems or along the course of the ureters bilaterally. The urinary bladder is partially filled with smooth walls.

Within the retroperitoneum, there is a well-circumscribed, hyperdense collection involving the left psoas muscle, consistent with a hematoma. There is no evidence of active extravasation of contrast (non-contrast study) or internal gas bubbles to suggest an infectious process. The right psoas muscle appears normal. The aorta and its major branches are patent, though there is mild atherosclerotic calcification noted at the bifurcation.

The bowel loops are non-dilated and show a normal distribution throughout the abdomen. There are a few small, benign-appearing diverticula noted in the sigmoid colon. The visualized lung bases are clear. There is mild degenerative change noted in the lumbar spine, characterized by marginal osteophytes and mild disc space narrowing.

**CONCLUSION:**
Left psoas hematoma without evidence of active bleeding or signs of infection.
