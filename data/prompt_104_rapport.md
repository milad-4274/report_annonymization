**INDICATION:** 55-year-old female presenting with upper abdominal pain and elevated lipase levels. Evaluate for pancreatitis and potential complications.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the abdomen and pelvis was performed following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous parenchymal attenuation; no focal masses or intrahepatic biliary dilatation is seen. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid, although a few small, nonobstructing choleliths are noted. The common bile duct is within normal limits for age.

The pancreas is diffusely enlarged and edematous, showing mild heterogeneous enhancement. There is characteristic stranding of the peripancreatic fat extending into the anterior pararenal space. However, there are no discrete fluid collections, no evidence of walled-off necrosis, and no vascular complications such as pseudoaneurysms or venous thrombosis identified. The pancreatic duct is not dilated.

The spleen and adrenal glands are unremarkable in appearance. The kidneys show symmetric enhancement and excretion without hydronephrosis or suspicious cortical lesions. A small, simple left renal cyst is noted, measuring approximately 1 cm.

The stomach and small bowel loops are non-dilated. The colon is normal in caliber without evidence of diverticulitis or obstructive patterns. There is a small amount of free fluid in the pelvis, which is likely physiologic. No pneumoperitoneum is identified.

The visualized lung bases are clear. The abdominal aorta is normal in caliber without aneurysmal dilation. Visualized bony structures are intact, though there is mild degenerative change noted at the L4-L5 facet joints.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are consistent with acute pancreatitis. There are no associated complications, such as necrosis or fluid collections.
