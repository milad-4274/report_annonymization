**INDICATION:** 77-year-old female presenting with right hip pain following low energy trauma. Status post right total hip arthroplasty three months ago. Evaluate for fracture.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT images were acquired through the pelvis and both proximal femurs without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were performed in the coronal and sagittal planes.

**FINDINGS:**
The visualized pelvic ring is intact. There is no evidence of acute fracture or dislocation involving the sacroiliac joints or the pubic symphysis. Mild degenerative changes are noted at the sacroiliac joints with small marginal osteophytes.

Regarding the right hip, there is complete dislocation of the prosthetic femoral head from the acetabular component. The femoral head is displaced superiorly and posteriorly relative to the acetabulum. The prosthetic components themselves appear intact; there is no evidence of periprosthetic fracture of the femur or the pelvis. No acute traumatic bony fragments are identified within the joint space. There is moderate soft tissue swelling surrounding the right hip joint.

The left hip demonstrates an intact native joint with mild narrowing of the joint space and small subchondral cysts in the acetabulum, consistent with mild osteoarthritis. No fracture or dislocation is seen on the left.

The visualized portions of the abdominal aorta and pelvic vasculature are unremarkable for the patient's age. There are a few small, benign-appearing uterine leiomyomas. The bladder is partially filled and appears unremarkable.

**CONCLUSION:**
Complete posterior dislocation of the right total hip arthroplasty. No associated periprosthetic fracture identified.
