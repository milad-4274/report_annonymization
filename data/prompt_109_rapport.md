**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 56-year-old male presenting with progressive abdominal fullness and a positive flow sign on physical examination. Evaluate for ascites, portal thrombosis, and liver dysmorphy.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were performed.

**Findings:**
The liver demonstrates a dysmorphic appearance characterized by surface nodularity and hypertrophy of the caudate lobe with corresponding atrophy of the right lobe. There is no focal hepatic mass identified. The portal vein is patent throughout its course; however, there is mild prominence of the periportal collateral vessels. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The common bile duct is within normal limits for age.

There is a large quantity of simple-appearing peritoneal free fluid distributed throughout the abdomen and pelvis, surrounding the liver and spleen and extending deep into the paracolic gutters and the pelvic cul-de-sac.

The spleen is mildly enlarged, measuring 13.5 cm in craniocaudal dimension. The pancreas appears unremarkable in morphology and attenuation, with no evidence of inflammatory changes or masses. The adrenal glands are bilateral and symmetric without nodules.

Both kidneys demonstrate normal enhancement and excretion. There is a small, non-obstructing 3 mm calculus noted in the lower pole of the left kidney. The urinary bladder is partially compressed by the surrounding ascites but otherwise shows smooth wall contours.

The bowel loops are generally non-dilated. There is a small amount of diverticulosis noted in the sigmoid colon without evidence of acute diverticulitis. The visualized portions of the thoracic aorta are normal in caliber. No significant lymphadenopathy is seen in the retroperitoneum or pelvic regions. The visualized osseous structures show mild degenerative changes of the lumbar spine.

**Conclusion:**
Dysmorphic liver consistent with chronic liver disease and a large volume of peritoneal ascites. No evidence of portal vein thrombosis.
