**INDICATION:** 54-year-old female presenting with upper abdominal pain and elevated lipase levels. Evaluate for pancreatitis and potential complications.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the abdomen and pelvis was performed following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous parenchymal attenuation; no focal masses or intrahepatic biliary dilatation is noted. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid, although a few small, nonobstructing choleliths are visualized. The common bile duct is within normal limits for age.

The pancreas is diffusely enlarged and edematous with mild blurring of the peripancreatic fat planes. There is some mild stranding in the anterior pararenal space. However, there is no evidence of focal necrosis, no organized fluid collections, and no pseudoaneurysms identified. The pancreatic duct is not dilated.

The spleen and adrenal glands are unremarkable in appearance. The kidneys show symmetric enhancement and excretion without hydronephrosis or suspicious masses. A small, simple cortical cyst is noted in the lower pole of the left kidney, measuring approximately 1 cm.

The stomach and small bowel loops are non-dilated. The colon is normal in caliber without wall thickening. There is a small amount of free fluid in the pelvis, which is likely physiologic. No pneumoperitoneum is seen. The aorta and its major branches are patent and of normal caliber. Visualized lung bases are clear.

**CONCLUSION:**
Findings are consistent with acute pancreatitis. There are no associated complications such as necrosis or fluid collections.
