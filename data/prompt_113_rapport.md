**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 88-year-old male, currently hospitalized for a spontaneous abdominal wall hematoma occurring in the setting of anticoagulant therapy. Evaluation for evolution of the hematoma.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the symphysis pubis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**Findings:**
The abdominal wall demonstrates a previously described hematoma located within the subcutaneous tissues of the anterior abdominal wall. This collection remains stable in size and attenuation compared to the prior study. There is no evidence of active extravasation of contrast material to suggest ongoing hemorrhage, nor are there any internal gas bubbles or rim enhancement to indicate an evolving infectious process.

The liver is normal in size and contour with homogeneous parenchymal attenuation; no focal lesions are identified. The gallbladder is unremarkable without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas demonstrate a normal appearance. The adrenal glands are symmetric and non-enlarged.

The kidneys show symmetric excretion of contrast. There is mild, bilateral cortical thinning and small, non-obstructing renal cysts, which are typical for the patient's age. No hydronephrosis is present.

The gastrointestinal tract is unremarkable. There is no evidence of bowel wall thickening or abnormal dilatation. A small amount of diverticulosis is noted in the sigmoid colon without signs of acute diverticulitis.

The urinary bladder is partially filled and appears smooth-walled. The pelvic organs are unremarkable for the patient's age.

There is no free intraperitoneal air or pleural effusion. Mild atherosclerotic calcifications are noted along the walls of the abdominal aorta and its primary branches. The visualized lung bases are clear.

**Conclusion:**
Stability of the known abdominal wall hematoma, without evidence of active bleeding or signs of superimposed infection.
