**INDICATION:** 39-year-old female presenting with fever and dyspnea. Question of occult pneumonia after negative chest X-ray.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging of the chest was performed without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The lungs are clear. There is no evidence of focal consolidation, centrilobular nodules, or ground-glass opacities to suggest pneumonia. The pleural spaces are clear without effusion or pneumothorax. The tracheobronchial tree is patent and unremarkable in caliber.

Regarding the mediastinum, there is an average-sized pericardial effusion noted, characterized by a circumferential layer of fluid surrounding the heart. There are no associated pericardial thickenings or enhancing nodules to suggest an infectious or inflammatory process. The heart size is otherwise within normal limits. The thoracic aorta and pulmonary arteries are normal in diameter. No significant mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy is identified.

The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and spleen, appear unremarkable. There is a small, simple hepatic cyst measuring approximately 8mm in the right lobe, which is an incidental finding. The bony structures of the thoracic cage are intact. A small amount of degenerative change is noted at the T7-T8 facet joints. The visualized soft tissues of the chest wall are normal.

**CONCLUSION:**
Average pericardial effusion without signs of infection.
