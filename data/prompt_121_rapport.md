**EXAMINATION:** MRI Abdomen (Liver Protocol)

**INDICATION:** 77-year-old female with history of hepatic transplant two years ago. Presenting with worsening cholestasis. Evaluate for bile duct dilatation, suspicious lesions, or anastomotic stricture.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar, multisequence imaging of the abdomen was performed including T1 and T2 weighted sequences, diffusion-weighted imaging, and MRCP sequences. Post-contrast dynamic imaging was obtained.

**FINDINGS:**
The transplanted liver demonstrates normal morphology and size with homogeneous signal intensity throughout the parenchyma. There is no evidence of focal mass lesions or suspicious enhancement patterns within the graft. The portal vein and hepatic veins are patent without evidence of thrombosis.

Regarding the biliary system, there is diffuse dilatation of the intra-hepatic bile ducts extending throughout both the right and left lobes. This dilatation persists down to the level of the biliary anastomosis, where an abrupt narrowing is noted. The extra-hepatic biliary tree distal to the anastomosis appears normal in caliber. No choledocholithiasis or filling defects are identified within the dilated segments.

The gallbladder is surgically absent. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance for the patient's age. The adrenal glands are normal. Both kidneys show mild cortical thinning and small, simple cortical cysts, which are non-specific and likely age-related. There is a small amount of free fluid noted in the perihepatic space, though no organized collections or abscesses are seen. The visualized bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall thickness. No significant lymphadenopathy is identified in the porta hepatis or retroperitoneum.

**CONCLUSION:**
Diffuse intra-hepatic bile duct dilatation proximal to the biliary anastomosis, consistent with an anastomotic stricture. No associated biliary leak or intra-abdominal abscess identified.
