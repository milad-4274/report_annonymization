**EXAMINATION:** MRI Abdomen (Liver Protocol)

**INDICATION:** 48-year-old male presenting with right upper quadrant pain, fever, and leukocytosis five days status post cholecystectomy. Evaluate for abscess or biloma.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar, multisequence imaging of the abdomen was performed. T1 and T2 weighted sequences were obtained along with diffusion-weighted imaging. Dynamic post-contrast images were acquired using an extracellular gadolinium-based agent, including dedicated MRCP sequences for biliary tree evaluation.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour. There is no focal hepatic mass or suspicious enhancement pattern noted. A few small, scattered T2-hyperintense foci are seen within the hepatic parenchyma, consistent with simple hepatic cysts. The portal vein and hepatic veins are patent without evidence of thrombosis.

Regarding the biliary system, there is mild dilatation of the intrahepatic bile ducts. The common bile duct is within normal limits for caliber. Following the administration of contrast, there is a clear extravasation of contrast material originating from the cystic duct stump into the gallbladder fossa. This collection is moderately sized and exhibits peripheral enhancement, consistent with a localized biloma. There is no evidence of a discrete, thick-walled abscess cavity or internal gas bubbles to suggest an infected collection at this time.

The pancreas is unremarkable in morphology and signal intensity, with no ductal dilatation. The spleen and adrenal glands are normal in appearance. Both kidneys show normal cortical thickness and excretion, though there is a small, non-obstructing 3mm calculus in the lower pole of the left kidney.

The visualized bowel loops are non-dilated. There is a moderate amount of free fluid in the perihepatic space and Morrison's pouch, likely reactive to the inflammatory process. No lymphadenopathy is identified in the porta hepatis or retroperitoneum. The abdominal wall is intact.

**CONCLUSION:**
Contrast leak from the cystic duct stump resulting in a localized biloma. No evidence of a discrete abscess.
