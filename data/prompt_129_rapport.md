**INDICATION:** 63-year-old male presenting with acute abdominal pain and elevated lactate levels. Evaluate for digestive ischemia.

**TECHNIQUE:** Computed tomography of the abdomen and pelvis was performed with intravenous contrast administration. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour without focal mass lesions. However, there is a complete lack of opacification within the main portal vein and both right and left portal branches, consistent with extensive acute thrombosis. The superior mesenteric vein also shows an intraluminal filling defect extending from its origin at the confluence of the splenic vein, indicating acute thrombosis. The splenic vein remains patent.

Regarding the bowel, there is diffuse, circumferential wall thickening involving the distal jejunum and the entirety of the ileum. These segments demonstrate a marked decrease in mucosal enhancement and associated interstitial edema, which is highly suggestive of mesenteric ischemia. There is a moderate amount of free fluid present in the paracolic gutters and around the liver. No evidence of pneumatosis intestinalis or portal venous gas is identified at this time.

The stomach is unremarkable in caliber and wall thickness. The ascending and descending colon appear normal, though there is some mild prominence of the transverse colon. The appendix is visualized and appears normal.

The pancreas, adrenals, and kidneys are unremarkable. No hydronephrosis is noted. The urinary bladder is moderately distended with smooth walls.

Incidental findings include mild degenerative changes of the lumbar spine with small osteophytes at L3-L4. There is a small, simple hepatic cyst measuring 1 cm in segment IVb, which is a common finding. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid.

The aorta is normal in caliber without aneurysmal dilatation. No lymphadenopathy is seen in the retroperitoneum or pelvic regions.

**CONCLUSION:**
Extensive acute thrombosis of the portal vein and superior mesenteric vein with associated signs of digestive ischemia involving the small bowel. No evidence of bowel perforation or pneumatosis.
