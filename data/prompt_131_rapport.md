**CT ANGIOGRAPHY (CTA) ABDOMEN AND PELVIS**

**Indication:** 78-year-old female, post-thrombectomy for stroke yesterday. Now presenting with acute onset abdominal pain. Rule out arterial dissection, bowel obstruction, or mesenteric ischemia.

**Technique:** Helical CT acquisition was performed from the diaphragm to the symphysis pubis. Contrast was administered intravenously via an automated injector with bolus tracking to optimize arterial phase opacification. Multiplanar reconstructions were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The liver is normal in size and contour without focal lesions; the gallbladder is unremarkable and no biliary ductal dilatation is noted. The spleen, pancreas, and adrenal glands demonstrate normal morphology and attenuation. There is no free fluid or free intraperitoneal air within the abdominal cavity.

Regarding the vascular assessment, the abdominal aorta is widely patent with mild atherosclerotic calcifications along the walls, but there is no evidence of dissection flap or aneurysmal dilatation. The celiac trunk, superior mesenteric artery, and renal arteries show normal opacification without evidence of stenosis or occlusion. There are no signs of bowel wall thickening or pneumatosis intestinalis to suggest digestive ischemia. The small and large bowel loops are non-dilated and follow a normal course, with no evidence of mechanical obstruction.

In the pelvis, the bladder is partially filled and unremarkable. There is a moderate amount of pelvic venous congestion. In the right groin, at the site of previous catheter access, there is a focal communication between the common femoral artery and the adjacent common femoral vein. This appears as a high-attenuation jet of contrast extending directly from the arterial lumen into the venous system, consistent with a post-puncture arteriovenous fistula. There is some mild soft tissue stranding surrounding the puncture site, though no large organized hematoma is seen.

Incidental findings include mild degenerative changes of the lumbar spine with marginal osteophytes and slight narrowing of the L4-L5 intervertebral disc space. Small, benign-appearing uterine leiomyomas are noted, which are expected for the patient's age.

**Conclusion:**
Post-puncture arteriovenous fistula involving the right common femoral artery and vein. No associated large hematoma or limb-threatening ischemia identified.
