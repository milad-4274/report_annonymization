**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 59-year-old female presenting with acute onset abdominal pain and elevated blood lactate levels. Evaluate for bowel ischemia or occlusion.

**Technique:** Helical CT imaging was performed from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous parenchymal attenuation; no focal masses or intrahepatic biliary dilatation is seen. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. The adrenal glands are symmetric and normal in size.

Regarding the vasculature, there is a short segment occlusion of the proximal superior mesenteric artery (SMA) immediately distal to its origin from the aorta. There is an abrupt termination of the contrast column. The celiac axis and the inferior mesenteric artery remain patent.

The stomach is normal in caliber. There are segments of the small bowel, particularly within the distribution of the SMA, demonstrating circumferential wall thickening and a lack of normal mucosal enhancement. There is a moderate amount of free fluid noted in the paracolic gutters and pelvis. No high-grade mechanical bowel obstruction is identified, though there are dilated loops of jejunum proximal to the affected area. The colon is unremarkable in course and caliber.

The kidneys show symmetric excretion of contrast without hydronephrosis. The urinary bladder is partially filled and unremarkable. Small, benign-appearing hepatic hemangioma is noted in segment IV. There are mild degenerative changes involving the lumbar spine and a small, simple left ovarian cyst measuring 1.2 cm.

**Conclusion:**
Short proximal occlusion of the superior mesenteric artery with associated findings consistent with bowel ischemia and accompanying peritoneal fluid.
