**EXAMINATION:** CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast

**INDICATION:** 76-year-old male with uncompensated cirrhosis. Evaluate for portal vein thrombosis, TIPS occlusion, and volume of ascites.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multidetector CT imaging was performed from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Arterial and portal venous phase images were acquired. Coronal and sagittal reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**

**Hepatobiliary:** The liver is small in size with a nodular surface contour, consistent with the known history of cirrhosis. There is mild atrophy of the right lobe and compensatory hypertrophy of the caudate lobe. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The intrahepatic biliary ducts are not dilated.

**TIPS and Vasculature:** The transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic (TIPS) shunt is in place. There is significant narrowing within the shunt lumen, appearing as subocclusive stenosis. Flow appears diminished, and there are early signs of shunt dysfunction. The main portal vein remains patent, though there is no evidence of acute portal vein thrombosis. However, there are prominent periportal collaterals and enlarged gastric varices noted, suggesting persistent portal hypertension. The splenic vein is patent but dilated.

**Spleen and Pancreas:** The spleen is enlarged, measuring approximately 15 cm in craniocaudal dimension. The pancreas demonstrates a normal lobulated contour and enhancement without focal masses or inflammatory changes.

**Adrenals and Kidneys:** Both adrenal glands are unremarkable. The kidneys show symmetric enhancement and excretion. There is a small, simple 4 mm cyst in the lower pole of the left kidney, which is an incidental finding. No hydronephrosis is seen.

**Gastrointestinal Tract:** There is a moderate amount of free fluid within the peritoneal cavity, most pronounced in the perihepatic space and the paracolic gutters, consistent with ascites. The stomach and small bowel loops are non-dilated. The appendix is visualized and appears normal.

**Pelvis and Musculoskeletal:** The urinary bladder is partially filled and smooth-walled. No pelvic lymphadenopathy is identified. There is mild degenerative change noted at the L4-L5 disc space with small marginal osteophytes. The visualized osseous structures otherwise demonstrate normal mineralization for the patient's age.

**CONCLUSION:**
Subocclusive stenosis of the TIPS shunt with early signs of dysfunction and associated features of portal hypertension. No portal vein thrombosis is identified. Moderate ascites is present.
