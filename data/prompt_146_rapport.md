**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 65-year-old male presenting with jaundice and laboratory evidence of cholestasis. Evaluate for biliary obstruction, pancreatic malignancy, or choledocholithiasis.

**Technique:** Helical CT imaging was performed from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour without focal mass lesions or suspicious enhancement patterns. There is diffuse, multifocal narrowing and stricturing involving both the intrahepatic and extrahepatic bile ducts. These strictures are interspersed with short segments of dilatation, creating a characteristic "beaded" appearance throughout the biliary tree. The gallbladder is present and moderately distended but shows no wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid; no radiopaque gallstones are identified within the lumen. The common bile duct is irregularly dilated.

The pancreas is normal in morphology and attenuation. There is no evidence of a focal pancreatic mass, no contour abnormality of the pancreatic head, and the main pancreatic duct is not dilated. The splenic vein and artery are patent.

The spleen and adrenal glands are unremarkable in size and appearance. The kidneys show symmetric excretion of contrast without hydronephrosis or renal calculi. There is a small, simple cortical cyst in the lower pole of the left kidney, measuring approximately 1 cm.

The gastrointestinal tract is non-dilated. There is a small amount of diverticulosis noted in the sigmoid colon without evidence of acute diverticulitis. No free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites is seen. The aorta is mildly ectatic at the infrarenal level, which is expected for the patient's age. The pelvic organs are unremarkable for a male of this age. Visualized lung bases are clear.

**Conclusion:**
Multifocal biliary strictures and segmental dilatations of the intra- and extrahepatic ducts, highly suggestive of primary sclerosing cholangitis. No evidence of obstructing choledocholithiasis or pancreatic malignancy.
