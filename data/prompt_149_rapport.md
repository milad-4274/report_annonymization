**INDICATION:** 55-year-old female with history of IV drug use presenting with left calf swelling and redness. Evaluate for abscess versus thrombosis.

**TECHNIQUE:** Computed tomography venography of the lower extremities was performed. Axial images were acquired from the level of the bilateral common iliac veins through the feet following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The pelvic veins, including the common iliac and external iliac veins, are patent bilaterally without evidence of filling defects. The common femoral, superficial femoral, and profunda femoris veins demonstrate normal opacification and caliber on both sides.

In the left lower extremity, there is an intraluminal filling defect noted within the popliteal vein, consistent with acute thrombosis. This thrombus extends slightly distally into the trifurcation. The associated soft tissues of the left popliteal fossa show mild inflammatory stranding, but there is no evidence of a drainable fluid collection or organized abscess. The calf veins, including the posterior tibial and peroneal veins, remain patent. On the right side, the popliteal and calf veins are unremarkable.

Regarding the musculoskeletal structures, there is mild degenerative change noted at the ankle joints bilaterally with small marginal osteophytes. There is a small, benign-appearing cortical cyst in the distal right tibia, which is an incidental finding. The visualized musculature of both lower limbs is symmetric and shows no signs of atrophy or abnormal enhancement. The skin and subcutaneous tissues of the right leg are normal; however, there is diffuse subcutaneous edema noted throughout the left calf.

**CONCLUSION:**
Acute left popliteal vein thrombosis. No evidence of associated abscess or deep tissue collection.
