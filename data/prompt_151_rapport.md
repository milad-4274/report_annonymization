**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 78-year-old female, post-thrombectomy yesterday for stroke. Now presenting with abdominal pain. Rule out arterial dissection, bowel obstruction, or digestive ischemia.

**Technique:** Helical CT acquisition was performed from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and attenuation with no focal lesions identified; the portal vein is patent. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The biliary tree is not dilated. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. The adrenal glands are symmetric and normal in morphology.

Regarding the vasculature, there is no evidence of aortic dissection or aneurysm. The celiac axis, superior mesenteric artery, and renal arteries show normal opacification without evidence of stenosis or occlusion. There are no signs of mesenteric venous thrombosis.

The stomach is normally distended. The small bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall enhancement throughout, with no evidence of pneumatosis or free intraperitoneal air. The ascending and transverse colon are unremarkable. However, there is a significant amount of retained fecal material within the descending colon and rectum, consistent with rectal fecal impaction. There is no associated pathological dilatation of the proximal colon, and no signs of digestive suffering such as wall edema or inflammatory stranding are noted.

The kidneys are normal in position and size with symmetric excretion of contrast. No hydronephrosis is seen. The urinary bladder is partially filled and unremarkable.

Incidental findings include mild atherosclerotic calcifications of the abdominal aorta and its primary branches. There are small, stable degenerative changes involving the lumbar facet joints and mild osteophytic spurring at L3-L4. A small, simple hepatic cyst is noted in segment IV, measuring approximately 1 cm.

**Conclusion:**
Rectal fecal impaction without evidence of pathological bowel dilatation or signs of digestive suffering.
