**CT ANGIOGRAPHY CHEST, ABDOMEN, AND PELVIS**

**Indication:** 77-year-old female. Control imaging one year following endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR). Evaluate for aneurysm exclusion and presence of endoleaks.

**Technique:** Helical CT acquisition was performed from the thoracic inlet through the symphysis pubis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Bolus tracking was utilized to optimize arterial opacification. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The thoracic aorta is normal in caliber without evidence of dissection or aneurysm. The heart size is within normal limits for age, although there is mild calcification noted along the mitral valve annulus. The lungs are clear without focal consolidation or pleural effusion, though there are a few scattered subpleural micronodules in the lower lobes, likely representing old granulomatous disease. The mediastinal contours are stable and no lymphadenopathy is identified.

In the abdomen, the previously placed endograft is well-positioned across the infrarenal abdominal aortic aneurysm. There is no evidence of graft migration. The proximal and distal fixation zones are secure. The aneurysmal sac demonstrates a decrease in maximum transverse diameter compared to the prior study, indicating positive remodeling and shrinkage of the sac. No contrast extravasation is seen within the sac or adjacent to the graft limbs to suggest Type I, II, or III endoleaks. The contralateral limb is patent and extends into the external iliac artery.

The liver, gallbladder, spleen, and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. Both kidneys show symmetric enhancement and excretion without hydronephrosis. There is a small, simple hepatic cyst measuring 1.2 cm in segment IV, which is an incidental finding. The adrenal glands are normal. The bowel loops are non-dilated and no free fluid or free air is noted within the peritoneal cavity. The visualized pelvic organs are unremarkable for the patient's age. The bony structures demonstrate generalized osteopenia and mild degenerative changes of the lumbar spine.

**Conclusion:**
Successful exclusion of the abdominal aortic aneurysm with evidence of sac shrinkage. No endoleak is identified.
