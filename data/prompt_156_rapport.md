**CT ANGIOGRAM CHEST**

**Indication:** 54-year-old male presenting with hemoptysis and dyspnea. Evaluate for pulmonary embolism versus lung cancer.

**Technique:** Helical CT imaging was performed from the thoracic inlet through the adrenal glands following the administration of intravenous contrast bolus timed for pulmonary arterial opacification. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are clear of any suspicious pulmonary nodules or masses; there is no evidence of primary bronchogenic carcinoma or metastatic disease. No pleural effusions or pneumothorax are identified. There is a small amount of dependent atelectasis in the posterior segments of both lower lobes, which is non-specific.

Regarding the pulmonary vasculature, there is a filling defect involving the left lower lobe pulmonary artery and its segmental branches, consistent with an acute lobar pulmonary embolism. The remaining lobar and segmental arteries are patent. The main pulmonary artery and right pulmonary artery are normal in caliber.

The heart is normal in size. The right ventricle is not enlarged, and the interventricular septum maintains its normal convexity toward the left ventricle. There is no evidence of right ventricular strain or cardiac dysfunction. The pericardium is thin and unremarkable.

The mediastinum demonstrates normal contours. The trachea and mainstem bronchi are patent. There is no significant mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy. The visualized esophagus is normal.

The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and spleen, are unremarkable. There is a small, simple hepatic cyst measuring 1.2 cm in segment IV, which is an incidental finding. The adrenal glands are normal in morphology. The bony structures of the thoracic cage show mild degenerative changes of the thoracic spine.

**Conclusion:**
Acute left lower lobe lobar pulmonary embolism without features of right cardiac dysfunction.
