**CT ANGIOGRAPHY (CTA) LOWER EXTREMITIES**

**Indication:** 68-year-old male presenting with lowered walking perimeter. Evaluate for arterial stenosis of the lower limbs.

**Technique:** Helical CT acquisition was performed from the level of the aortic bifurcation to the ankles following the intravenous administration of non-ionic iodinated contrast medium. Bolus tracking was utilized to optimize arterial opacification. Multiplanar reformations and maximum intensity projections were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The abdominal aorta is widely patent at its bifurcation without evidence of aneurysmal dilatation. Both common iliac arteries and internal/external iliac arteries demonstrate normal caliber and opacification, showing no hemodynamically significant stenosis or occlusive disease.

Moving distally, the common femoral, superficial femoral, and popliteal arteries are patent bilaterally. There is mild, eccentric wall calcification noted along the course of the superficial femoral arteries, but the luminal diameter remains preserved.

In the infra-popliteal segment, there are multiple areas of high-grade stenosis involving both the fibular and posterior tibial arteries. These stenoses are multifocal and associated with irregular wall thickening and atherosclerotic plaque. The anterior tibial arteries remain patent, although some mild wall irregularity is noted.

Regarding non-vascular structures, there is mild degenerative change noted in the visualized portions of the hip joints, characterized by slight joint space narrowing and small marginal osteophytes. A few small, benign-appearing cortical cysts are present within the femoral heads. The visualized musculature of the thighs and calves is symmetric and shows no evidence of atrophy or abnormal fluid collections. The subcutaneous soft tissues are unremarkable.

**Conclusion:**
Multifocal high-grade stenosis of the bilateral fibular and posterior tibial arteries. No evidence of complete arterial occlusion or acute limb-threatening ischemia.
