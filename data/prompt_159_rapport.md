**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 59-year-old female presenting with chronic abdominal pain. Patient reports no specific association with diet. Evaluate for abnormality explaining the pain.

**Technique:** Helical CT imaging was performed from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour with homogeneous parenchymal attenuation; there is no focal mass or intrahepatic biliary ductal dilatation. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance. The adrenal glands are symmetric and normal in size.

Regarding the vasculature, there is significant narrowing of the proximal superior mesenteric artery (SMA) origin, consistent with high-grade stenosis. There is evidence of compensatory collateralization, specifically noted via enlargement of the pancreaticoduodenal arcades originating from the celiac trunk to bypass the SMA stenosis. The celiac axis remains patent. The aorta is normal in caliber without aneurysmal dilatation.

The kidneys show symmetric enhancement and excretion without hydronephrosis. A small, simple cortical cyst is noted in the lower pole of the left kidney, measuring approximately 1 cm. The urinary bladder is well-distended with smooth walls.

The gastrointestinal tract shows no evidence of bowel wall thickening, obstruction, or inflammatory changes. There is a small amount of diverticula within the sigmoid colon without signs of acute diverticulitis. No free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites is identified.

The visualized lung bases are clear. There are no enlarged retroperitoneal or pelvic lymph nodes. The visualized osseous structures are intact, though there is mild degenerative change noted at the L4-L5 facet joints.

**Conclusion:**
High-grade stenosis of the superior mesenteric artery, which is currently compensated by collateral flow from the celiac trunk. No evidence of mesenteric ischemia or bowel infarction.
