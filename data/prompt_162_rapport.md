**CT ABDOMEN AND PELVIS WITH IV CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 84-year-old male with severe dementia presenting with diffuse lower abdominal pain. Limited clinical history provided. Rule out appendicitis or mesenteric ischemia.

**Technique:** Axial CT images were acquired from the diaphragm to the symphysis pubis following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed.

**Findings:**
The liver demonstrates normal size and contour without focal mass lesions; however, there is mild hepatic steatosis noted. The gallbladder is present without wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The biliary tree is not dilated. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance for the patient's age.

Regarding the gastrointestinal tract, the stomach and small bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall enhancement throughout. There is no evidence of pneumatosis intestinalis or portal venous gas to suggest mesenteric ischemia. The appendix is visualized, measuring 5 mm in diameter, without surrounding inflammatory stranding or fluid collections, making appendicitis unlikely. The colon is unremarkable without evidence of diverticulitis or obstructing masses.

The kidneys show symmetric excretion of contrast. There is a small, 3 mm non-obstructing calculus in the left lower pole. No hydronephrosis is seen. The urinary bladder is partially filled and appears smooth-walled.

Evaluation of the pelvic bones reveals a single, well-defined lytic lesion located within the right iliac wing. This lesion is associated with focal cortical destruction at the lateral margin. The remaining visualized osseous structures show generalized osteopenia and mild degenerative changes of the lumbar spine and sacroiliac joints.

There is no free intraperitoneal air or significant ascites. The visualized lung bases are clear.

**Conclusion:**
Single lytic lesion in the right iliac wing with associated cortical destruction. No associated soft tissue component or pathologic fracture identified.
