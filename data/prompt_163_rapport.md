**INDICATION:** 79-year-old female presenting with left hip pain following minor trauma. Evaluate for fracture.

**TECHNIQUE:** Helical CT imaging was performed through the pelvis and both proximal femurs. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The pelvic girdle is visualized. There is no evidence of acute fracture or dislocation involving the sacroiliac joints or the pubic symphysis. The visualized pelvic organs are unremarkable for the patient's age, although there is mild degenerative change noted at the level of the sacrum.

Regarding the hips, the right hip joint space is well preserved without significant osteoarthritis. The right femoral head and neck appear intact. On the left side, however, there is a large, aggressive lytic lesion centered in the proximal left femur. This lesion demonstrates significant cortical destruction and is associated with an adjacent soft tissue mass extending into the surrounding musculature. While the clinical history mentions trauma, the morphology of this lesion is highly suspicious for malignancy. There is no clear displaced fracture line identified, though the bone is severely attenuated in the affected area.

The bladder is partially filled and appears unremarkable. There are some small, benign-appearing calcifications within the pelvic vasculature, consistent with mild atherosclerosis. The visualized bowel loops are non-dilated and show no wall thickening.

**CONCLUSION:**
Lytic lesion in the proximal left femur with associated cortical destruction and soft tissue mass, which is highly suspicious for malignancy. No acute displaced fracture is identified.
