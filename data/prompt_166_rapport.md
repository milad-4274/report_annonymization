**INDICATION:** 54-year-old male presenting with atraumatic mandible pain. Evaluate for fracture versus lesion.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT images were acquired through the neck and mandible without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were performed in the coronal and sagittal planes.

**FINDINGS:**
The visualized portions of the cervical spine demonstrate mild degenerative changes, specifically some marginal osteophyte formation at the C5-C6 level, which is expected for the patient's age. The soft tissues of the neck are symmetric without evidence of gross lymphadenopathy or abnormal fluid collections. The thyroid gland appears normal in size and attenuation.

Regarding the mandible, there is a large, poorly defined expansile lesion involving the right mandibular body and angle. This lesion demonstrates internal lucency with an aggressive appearance, showing clear breach of the inferior cortical margin. When compared to previous imaging from three weeks ago, there is evidence of rapid interval growth in both the transverse and longitudinal dimensions. There is no evidence of an acute traumatic fracture, although the cortex is significantly thinned by the underlying process.

The remaining mandible, including the symphysis and left side, remains intact with normal marrow signal. The temporomandibular joints are unremarkable. The visualized upper airway is patent. Small, benign-appearing calcifications are noted within the carotid arteries, consistent with mild atherosclerosis.

**CONCLUSION:**
Expansile lesion of the right mandible with associated cortical breach and rapid interval growth. No acute fracture identified.
