**INDICATION:** 32-year-old female presenting with left proximal humerus pain for three weeks. Prior radiographs demonstrate a lytic lesion. Evaluation for nature of lesion and suspicion for neoplasm.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar, multisequence imaging of the left shoulder was performed without intravenous contrast. Sequences include T1-weighted, T2-weighted fat-saturated, and STIR images.

**FINDINGS:**
The glenohumeral joint is well aligned. There is no evidence of joint effusion or significant synovial thickening. The glenoid labrum appears intact without evidence of tear or detachment.

Regarding the musculature, the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, and subscapularis tendons are unremarkable in signal and morphology, with no signs of high-grade partial or full-thickness tears. The biceps tendon is centered within the bicipital groove and shows normal signal intensity.

There is a focal abnormality noted within the bone. Specifically, there is an eccentric, lytic lesion located in the medullary space of the proximal left humerus. This lesion lacks a defined sclerotic margin and demonstrates ill-defined borders. Associated marrow edema is present, extending into the adjacent cortex, along with surrounding soft tissue edema in the peri-lesional region.

Incidental findings include a small, benign-appearing paralabral cyst in the posterior joint capsule. Additionally, there is mild degenerative change noted at the acromioclavicular joint with minimal hypertrophic osteophyte formation. The visualized portions of the clavicle and scapula are otherwise unremarkable.

**CONCLUSION:**
Eccentric lytic lesion in the proximal left humerus with associated soft tissue edema and lack of a sclerotic margin, which is highly suspicious for a neoplastic process. No pathologic fracture is identified.
