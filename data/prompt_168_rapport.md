**CT CHEST WITH CONTRAST**

**Indication:** 57-year-old male presenting with left-sided thoracic pain. Cardiac etiology has been ruled out. Prior chest X-ray demonstrated irregularity of the left 6th rib. Evaluate for rib anomaly.

**Technique:** Helical CT acquisition was performed from the thoracic inlet through the adrenal glands following the administration of intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were reviewed.

**Findings:**
The lungs are clear without evidence of focal consolidation, suspicious pulmonary nodules, or interstitial lung disease. There is no pleural effusion or pneumothorax identified. The trachea and mainstem bronchi remain patent.

The mediastinal and hilar contours are within normal limits. No significant lymphadenopathy is noted in the axillary or mediastinal stations. The heart size is normal and there is no pericardial effusion. The thoracic aorta and great vessels demonstrate normal caliber and course.

Regarding the skeletal structures, there is an aggressive lytic lesion involving the posterior aspect of the left 6th rib. This lesion demonstrates cortical destruction with associated soft tissue extension into the adjacent chest wall and focal pleural thickening overlying the site of the lesion. The remaining visualized ribs, clavicles, and thoracic spine show no similar destructive processes.

Incidental findings include mild degenerative changes of the thoracic vertebral bodies with small marginal osteophytes. There is a small, stable hiatal hernia noted. The visualized upper abdominal organs, including the liver and spleen, appear unremarkable for the patient's age.

**Conclusion:**
Aggressive lytic lesion of the left 6th rib with associated soft tissue extension and adjacent pleural thickening, which is highly concerning for malignancy.
