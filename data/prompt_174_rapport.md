**INDICATION:** 65-year-old male presenting with right hip pain without history of trauma. Prior radiographs demonstrate moderate hip osteoarthritis and a lytic lesion of the right femoral neck. Evaluate nature of the lesion and assess for pathological fracture.

**TECHIQUE:** Helical CT imaging was performed through the pelvis and both proximal femurs. Multiplanar reformatted images were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The pelvic girdle is intact. There is no evidence of acute fracture or dislocation within the sacroiliac joints or the pubic symphysis. The visualized pelvic organs are unremarkable for a study of this modality, though there is some mild prostatic enlargement noted.

Regarding the hips, there is bilateral hip osteoarthritis characterized by joint space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, and small marginal osteophytes, which is more pronounced on the right side. On the right, there is a large, aggressive-appearing osteolytic focus involving the right proximal femur, specifically centered in the femoral neck and extending into the intertrochanteric region. This lesion demonstrates cortical thinning and focal destruction of the superior cortex. While there is no complete cortical break or displacement, the remaining bone stock is severely compromised, indicating an impending pathological fracture. No associated soft tissue mass or significant fluid collection is seen surrounding the lesion.

The left proximal femur remains intact without similar lytic lesions or suspicious bone destruction. The visualized portions of the lumbar spine show mild degenerative disc disease at L4-L5 and L5-S1 with small uncovertebral osteophytes. No suspicious blastic or lytic lesions are identified in the visualized axial skeleton.

**CONCLUSION:**
Large osteolytic lesion of the right proximal femur with significant cortical destruction and an impending pathological fracture.
