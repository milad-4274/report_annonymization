**INDICATION:** 84-year-old male with severe dementia presenting with diffuse lower abdominal pain. Clinical history is limited. Evaluate for appendicitis or mesenteric ischemia.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT imaging was performed through the abdomen and pelvis without intravenous contrast. Coronal and sagittal reformations were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The liver demonstrates a normal size and contour with smooth margins; no focal hepatic lesions are identified. The gallbladder is present without evidence of wall thickening or pericholecystic fluid. The spleen and pancreas are unremarkable in appearance for the patient's age.

Regarding the gastrointestinal tract, there is no evidence of appendiceal enlargement or surrounding inflammatory stranding to suggest appendicitis. The small bowel loops are non-dilated and demonstrate normal wall thickness throughout the abdomen and pelvis. There is no evidence of pneumatosis intestinalis or portal venous gas to suggest mesenteric ischemia. The colon is unremarkable without signs of diverticulitis or obstructive patterns.

The kidneys are symmetric in size and position. There is mild bilateral cortical atrophy and a few small, simple renal cysts bilaterally, which are common for this age group. No hydronephrosis or radiopaque calculi are seen. The urinary bladder is partially filled and appears unremarkable.

Evaluation of the musculoskeletal system reveals an acute, minimally displaced fracture involving the right iliac wing. The left ilium, sacrum, and coccyx remain intact. There is mild degenerative change noted at the L4-L5 and L5-S1 intervertebral disc spaces with associated marginal osteophyte formation. The visualized pelvic joints are otherwise stable.

No free intraperitoneal air or significant pleural effusions are identified. No lymphadenopathy is noted in the retroperitoneum or pelvic regions.

**CONCLUSION:**
Acute fracture of the right iliac wing without significant displacement. No associated pelvic hematoma or dislocation.
