**EXAMINATION:** CT Right Ankle / Distal Leg

**INDICATION:** 18-year-old male presenting with rising right tibial pain for two months. Patient reports prior radiographs at PCP were negative for anomaly. Evaluate for osteomyelitis or suspicious bone lesion.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar reformatted images were obtained through the right distal leg and ankle joint without intravenous contrast. Coronal and sagittal reconstructions were performed.

**FINDINGS:**
The visualized portions of the distal tibial and fibular shafts demonstrate normal cortical thickness and medullary signal. There is no evidence of aggressive periosteal reaction, cortical destruction, or focal lucent lesions to suggest osteomyelitis or an underlying primary bone malignancy.

Regarding the ankle joint, there is a spiral fracture of the distal fibula extending above the syndesmosis, consistent with a Weber B pattern. The fracture line is well-defined without significant displacement of the main fragments. The distal tibial epiphysis and the medial malleolus remain intact. The tibiotalar joint space is preserved, and there is no evidence of talar shift or widening of the syndesmosis.

The visualized soft tissues around the ankle and distal leg are unremarkable. There is a small amount of mild subcutaneous edema surrounding the fibular fracture site, likely representing chronic post-traumatic change given the clinical history. A small, non-specific accessory ossicle is noted posterior to the talus, consistent with an os trigonum, which is a common anatomic variant. Additionally, there is a mild prominence of the anterior process of the calcaneus, though it does not appear acutely fractured. The visualized tendons and ligaments are grossly intact within the limits of non-contrast CT.

**CONCLUSION:**
Weber B fracture of the right distal fibula. No evidence of associated syndesmotic widening or dislocation. No CT evidence of osteomyelitis or suspicious osseous lesions.
