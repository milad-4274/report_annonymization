**INDICATION:** 64-year-old female who suffered a fall from standing height. Presents with intense pelvic pain. Evaluate for pelvic fracture and active hemorrhage.

**TECHNIQUE:** Helical CT imaging of the pelvis was performed without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformats were reviewed.

**FINDINGS:**
The bony pelvis is visualized. There is an acute, non-displaced fracture involving the left ischiopubic ramus. The pubic symphysis remains aligned and there is no evidence of diastasis. The sacroiliac joints are symmetric and intact bilaterally. Both hip joints demonstrate preserved joint spaces; however, there are mild marginal osteophytes at the superior acetabular rims bilaterally, consistent with mild degenerative joint disease. No dislocation or subluxation of the femoral heads is noted.

Regarding the soft tissues, there is no evidence of a large pelvic hematoma or active extravasation of contrast (study performed non-contrast). The bladder is moderately distended and appears unremarkable without wall thickening. The prostate is not applicable. The rectum is unremarkable. Small bowel loops within the pelvis show normal caliber and wall thickness.

There are small, benign-appearing uterine leiomyomas noted, which are common for the patient's age group. No free fluid or pneumoperitoneum is identified within the dependent portions of the pelvic cavity. The visualized lower ureters are not dilated. The visualized portions of the lumbar spine show mild disc space narrowing at L5-S1.

**CONCLUSION:**
Acute fracture of the left ischiopubic ramus. No evidence of active bleeding or associated pelvic hematoma.
