**INDICATION:** 54-year-old male presenting with left-sided mandible pain following facial trauma. Evaluate for fracture or dislocation.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT images were acquired through the neck and lower face without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar coronal and sagittal reformations were performed.

**FINDINGS:**
The visualized portions of the skull base and facial bones are reviewed. There is a displaced fracture involving the body of the left mandible, located lateral to the mental foramen. The fracture line extends superiorly and posteriorly, with slight medial displacement of the anterior fragment. No extension of the fracture into the symphysis or across the midline is noted.

The bilateral mandibular condyles are normally positioned within the glenoid fossae; there is no evidence of dislocation or condylar neck fracture. The coronoid processes and mandibular rami remain intact bilaterally.

The temporomandibular joints appear unremarkable. The soft tissues of the floor of the mouth show mild edema adjacent to the fracture site, but no large hematoma or foreign bodies are identified. The airway is patent, though there is slight deviation of the tongue toward the right.

Regarding incidental findings, there is mild degenerative change noted at the C2-C3 facet joints. A small, stable-appearing calcified carotid artery plaque is noted at the origin of the left internal carotid artery. The thyroid gland is normal in size and attenuation. The visualized cervical vertebrae maintain normal alignment.

**CONCLUSION:**
Acute displaced fracture of the body of the left mandible. No associated mandibular dislocation.
