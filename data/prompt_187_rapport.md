**INDICATION:** 32-year-old female presenting with right shoulder trauma. Examination performed for pre-surgical planning.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial MDCT images were acquired through the right shoulder joint without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar coronal and sagittal reformations were performed.

**FINDINGS:**
The visualized portions of the thoracic cage and the right clavicle are intact without evidence of acute fracture or dislocation. The acromioclavicular joint is well-maintained with no widening or superior displacement noted.

Regarding the glenohumeral joint, there is a severely displaced, comminuted fracture involving the surgical neck of the right humerus. Multiple bony fragments are identified at the fracture site, with significant medial and posterior displacement of the humeral shaft relative to the humeral head. There is an associated loss of normal anatomical alignment. The articular surface of the humeral head remains intact, though it is displaced from its normal relationship with the shaft. The glenoid fossa appears smooth and unremarkable, with no evidence of a glenoid rim fracture or bony Bankart lesion.

The surrounding soft tissues show expected swelling and edema adjacent to the fracture site. There is a small amount of joint effusion present. A small, non-obstructing calcification is noted within the supraspinatus tendon insertion, likely representing a chronic minor tendinopathy. The visualized musculature of the rotator cuff is symmetric in bulk. The visualized lung apices are clear.

**CONCLUSION:**
Comminuted and displaced multi-fragmentary fracture of the right humeral neck. No associated glenoid fracture or dislocation of the humeral head from the glenoid fossa.
