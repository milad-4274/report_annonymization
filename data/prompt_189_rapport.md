**EXAMINATION:** MRI LEFT KNEE

**INDICATION:** 22-year-old female presenting with left knee pain for four weeks. Initial radiographs were negative for acute fracture or focal lesion. Evaluate for occult fracture or suspicious femoral bony lesion.

**TECHNIQUE:** Multiplanar, multisequence imaging was performed through the left knee without the administration of intravenous contrast.

**FINDINGS:**
**Bones and Joints:** There is a well-defined osteochondral fracture involving the weight-bearing surface of the left lateral femoral condyle. This is characterized by a small fragment of articular cartilage and underlying subchondral bone that is partially detached, associated with localized marrow edema within the lateral femoral condysle. No other occult fractures or stress reactions are identified in the femur, tibia, or fibula. The joint space is preserved, although there is a mild joint effusion present.

**Ligaments:** The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) and posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) demonstrate normal signal intensity and morphology, with intact fibers throughout their course. The medial collateral ligament (MCL) and lateral collateral ligament (LCL) complexes are unremarkable.

**Menisci:** Both the medial and lateral menisci are intact, showing normal wedge-shaped morphology without evidence of tear or degeneration.

**Soft Tissues and Patellofemoral Compartment:** The patella is normally aligned. There is a very mild thickening of the patellar tendon, likely representing a physiological variant. A small amount of synovial thickening is noted around the suprapatellar bursa. The surrounding musculature and subcutaneous soft tissues are otherwise unremarkable.

**CONCLUSION:**
Osteochondral fracture of the left lateral femoral condyle. No associated displaced intra-articular loose bodies are identified.
