**INDICATION:** 67-year-old male presenting with pelvic trauma following a fall from a horse. Evaluate for sacral fracture or bony lesions.

**TECHNIQUE:** Helical CT imaging of the pelvis was performed without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were reviewed in the axial, sagittal, and coronal planes.

**FINDINGS:**
The pelvic ring is generally intact. There is a characteristic "H-shaped" fracture pattern involving the sacrum, consisting of bilateral vertical fractures through the sacral ala extending inferiorly through the sacral foramina, connected by a transverse fracture line across the sacral hiatus. There is minimal posterior displacement of the central sacral fragment. The sacroiliac joints are otherwise congruent without widening or dislocation.

The hip joints are well maintained. There is mild marginal osteophyte formation at the superior and medial aspects of both femoral heads, consistent with mild degenerative joint disease. The acetabula are intact without evidence of fracture.

The pubic rami and symphysis pubis are unremarkable; no acute fractures are identified in the anterior pelvic ring.

Visualized portions of the lumbar spine show mild multilevel disc space narrowing and small endplate osteophytes, which are age-appropriate changes. No suspicious lytic or blastic bony lesions are seen throughout the visualized pelvic girdle.

The pelvic soft tissues are grossly unremarkable, although there is some expected subcutaneous edema and hematoma surrounding the posterior sacral region. The bladder is partially filled and appears unremarkable. No free intraperitoneal air or large pelvic collections are noted.

**CONCLUSION:**
Acute H-type fracture of the sacrum. No associated sacroiliac joint dislocation.
