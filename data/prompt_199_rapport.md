**MRI LUMBAR SPINE**

**Indication:** 45-year-old female presenting with lower back pain and radiation into the right leg. Evaluate for disc herniation.

**Technique:** Multiplanar, multisequence imaging of the lumbar spine was performed without the administration of intravenous contrast. Sagittal T1, T2, and STIR sequences were obtained, along with axial T2 weighted images through the lumbar intervertebral discs.

**Findings:**
The alignment of the lumbar vertebral bodies is maintained without evidence of spondylolisthesis. The vertebral body heights are preserved throughout the lumbar region. Marrow signal intensity is within normal limits for the patient's age, although there is a small, well-circumscribed hemangioma noted within the body of L3 which is an incidental finding.

The conus medullaris terminates at the L1 level and demonstrates normal morphology and signal intensity. The cauda equina nerve roots are normally distributed within the thecal sac. There is no evidence of an intradural mass or collection.

At L1-L2, the intervertebral disc height is maintained. There is no significant bulging or protrusion of the disc material. The central canal and neural foramina are patent.

At L2-L3, there is a mild circumferential disc bulge associated with a small right paracentral protrusion. This combination of disc bulging and facet joint hypertrophy results in moderate right-sided foraminal stenosis, causing narrowing of the exit zone for the L2 nerve root. The central canal remains widely patent.

At L3-L4, the disc shows a slight loss of hydration but maintains adequate height. No focal disc herniation is identified. The neural foramina and central canal are open.

At L4-L5, there is a mild diffuse disc bulge. There is no significant impingement upon the traversing or exiting nerve roots. The facets show minimal degenerative changes.

At L5-S1, the disc space is preserved. No disc extrusion or sequestration is seen. The thecal sac is unremarkable.

The visualized portions of the right and left psoas muscles are symmetric and show no abnormal fluid collections or masses. There is a very small amount of facet joint effusion at L4-L5, likely representing mild degenerative change.

**Conclusion:**
Right-sided L2-L3 foraminal stenosis of multiple origins, resulting from a combination of a small disc protrusion and facet hypertrophy. No evidence of central canal stenosis or cauda equina compression.
