**INDICATION:** 53-year-old male presenting with painful swelling of the left knee. Evaluate for septic arthritis versus osteoarthritis.

**TECHNIQUE:** Axial CT images were acquired through the left knee joint without intravenous contrast. Multiplanar reformations were performed in the sagittal and coronal planes.

**FINDINGS:**
The bony alignment of the left knee is maintained. There is evidence of tri-compartmental osteoarthritis. In the medial compartment, there is marked narrowing of the joint space associated with subchondral sclerosis and marginal osteophyte formation. Similar though less severe degenerative changes are noted within the lateral compartment and the patellofemoral joint, characterized by mild joint space narrowing and small osteophytes at the poles of the patella.

There is a small amount of joint effusion present within the suprapatellar bursa. No aggressive periosteal reaction or cortical destruction is seen to suggest an underlying infectious process or malignancy. The visualized soft tissues surrounding the joint are unremarkable, without significant edema or fluid collections.

The distal femur and proximal tibia demonstrate normal mineralization for the patient's age. A small, benign-appearing cortical enclosing bone island is noted in the posterior aspect of the distal femoral metaphysis. There is a mild, non-specific flattening of the tibial plateau which likely represents a chronic remodeling variant. The fibular head is intact and normally positioned.

**CONCLUSION:**
Left knee tri-compartmental osteoarthritis with a small associated articular effusion. No CT evidence of septic arthritis.
